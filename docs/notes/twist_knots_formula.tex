\section{Summary}

We consider the twist knots $K_n$ (Conway notation $[n\,2]$)
\[
3_1,\ 4_1,\ 5_2,\ 6_1,\ 7_2,\ 8_1,\ 9_2,\ 10_1,\ 11a_{247},\ 12a_{803}
\]
as the family $\Tw_m$ of antiparallel double braids with $m$ full twists (Section~\ref{sec:conv}), and ask for the
reduced colored HOMFLY polynomial $H_R(\Tw_m)$ in a non-rectangular representation $R$, explicit in the twist
parameter.

\paragraph{The final form.}  Everything below combines into one conjecture for every $R$ and every $m$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:final}, stated with all its ingredients in Section~\ref{sec:onepage}):
\[
H_R(\Tw_m)=1+\sum_Xw_X(R)\bigl(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1\bigr),\qquad
w(R)=\sum_{\substack{(c,M):\ M\subseteq R\\ M\not\supseteq[4,3,1],\,[3,2,2,1]}}Z_c\,\cf Rc\,\Et_c\;+\;t(R).
\]
The first sum is the closed channel formula below the two minimal ghost diagrams $[4,3,1]$, $[3,2,2,1]$ (one term per
channel and minimal diagram; every diagram with ghosts contains one of the two).  Everything above them (the ghosts, the
multiplicity-two block of $[4,3,2,1]$, the diagrams with several ghosts, the deferred terms) is the single vector $t(R)$
in the vanishing ideals $\sum_{U\subseteq R,\,U\supseteq[4,3,1]\text{ or }[3,2,2,1]}I_U$ for which every coefficient
obeys the \emph{regularity law}: $w_X(R)/(d_X\rho(R))$ is a polynomial in $A^2$ of degree at most $|R|-|M_X(R)|$,
$M_X(R)$ the smallest minimal diagram of $X$ inside $R$, and $w_X(R)=0$ for a channel absent at $R$.  This is a finite
linear system with closed coefficients.  The law holds on all exact weights up to six boxes ($263/263$, the bound attained
wherever it is positive), and as a solver it has a unique solution in every case tried, which reproduces label by label
every verified twist vector at nine and ten boxes at both points, Formula~I at $[4,3,2,1]$, the other branch's
eleven-box files, transposition at six pairs of diagrams up to eleven boxes and Rosso--Jones, with no trefoil rule, zero
rule or fitted input.  The rest of this summary lists the pieces in the order in which they were found.

\paragraph{The universal formula.}  The answer is one conjectural formula for every $R$ and every $m\in\mathbb Z$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:U}, Section~\ref{sec:universal}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Usum}
\boxed{\;H_R(\Tw_m)=\sum_{c\in\{\vac\}\cup\Chan(R)}Z_c\,\cf Rc\,\Phi_c(m)\;}
\qquad\Bigl(+\sum_{g\in\Gh(R)}Z_g\,\cf Rg\,\Phi_g(m)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where the second sum is present only when $R$ contains a ghost diagram.
The first sum runs over the vacuum and the channels $c$ of $R\otimes\Rbar$, i.e.\ the composites $[Z,Z']$ with $Z,Z'\subseteq R$
(a conjugate pair $[Z,Z']$, $[Z',Z]$ counted once).  Each term is a product of three factors: a \emph{colour binomial}
$\cf Rc$, which depends only on $R$; a \emph{twist binomial} $\Phi_c(m)$, which depends only on $m$; and a constant $Z_c$ of
the channel.  Both binomials come from one lower triangular matrix $N$, the \emph{binomial matrix}, which expands the
normalised composite characters $\chih_X(R)=\chi_X(R)/d_X$ and the powers of the twist eigenvalues
$\Lam_X=A^{2|Z|}q^{2(\kappa_Z+\kappa_{Z'})}$ in the same way (a two-sided binomial theorem):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twobinomS}
\chih_X(R)=\sum_cN_{Xc}\,\rho(R_c)\,\cf Rc,\qquad\qquad \Lam_X^{\,m}=\sum_cN_{Xc}\,\Phi_c(m),
\end{equation}
with $R_c$ the minimal diagram of $c$ and $\rho$ the closed product \eqref{eq:topweight}.  Two normalisations fix $N$
(away from the ghost directions of Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}): $\cf Rc=0$ whenever $c$ is absent from $R\otimes\Rbar$, and
$\Phi_c(-1)=1$.  A third one is then a law and not a choice: $\cf{R_c}c=1$, because on the vanishing ideal of a diagram
$U$ the evaluation at $U$ is $\rho(U)$ times the twist functional at $m=-1$ (the ideal identity,
Observation~\ref{obs:monomial}).  So the multiplicity $m_c(R_c)$ of $c$ in $R_c\otimes\Rbar_c$, which is $2$ for some
separate/separate pairs (all two-cell ones, the first three in $[4,3,2,1]$), is not carried by a term of
\eqref{eq:Usum}; it comes from the block of the diagram (Section~\ref{sec:cubic}).  The constants are products
of quantum numbers and factors $D_k=Aq^k-A^{-1}q^{-k}$ for every channel whose diagonal $N_{cc}$ is closed (Table~\ref{tab:N}),
\begin{gather*}
Z_c=d_c\,N_{cc}\,\rho(R_c),\qquad\text{e.g.}\quad Z_{[1]}=D_1D_{-1},\qquad Z_{[k]}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}D_{k+i}D_{i-1},\\
Z_{[2,1]}=D_{-3}D_{-2}D_{-1}D_1D_2D_3,\qquad Z_{\{[1,1],[2]\}}=-[3]^2\{q\}^4D_{-2}D_2,
\end{gather*}
and $Z_c$ is the contribution of $c$ to the figure-eight polynomial at $R=R_c$: at $m=-1$ the formula reads
$H_R(4_1)=\sum_cZ_c\cf Rc$.  For $R=[r]$ the colour binomial is a quantum binomial,
$\cf{[r]}{[k]}=\binom rk_q\prod_{i<k}D_{r+i}/D_{k+i}$, and for rectangular $R$ (singles only) \eqref{eq:Usum} is the
cyclotomic expansion of Kameyama--Nawata--Tao--Zhang (Section~\ref{sec:single}).  For non-rectangular $R$ the pair channels
$\{Z,Z'\}$, $Z\ne Z'$, are the new terms.  The ghost terms occur only when $R$ contains a ghost diagram (the first are
$[4,3,1]$ and $[3,2,2,1]$, at eight boxes); they have the same three-factor form, and $Z_g$ is fixed by the trefoil
(Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}).  Table~\ref{tab:N} lists the closed rule for every block of $N$ and the blocks that are still
open.  The items below give the pieces in the order in which they were found.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Explicit in $m$ (evolution form).}  For every $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:evol}
H_R(\Tw_m)\;=\;1+\sum_{X\in\Chan(R)} w_X(R)\,\bigl(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1\bigr),\qquad
\Lam_{[Z,Z']}=A^{2|Z|}q^{\,2(\kappa_Z+\kappa_{Z'})},
\end{equation}
where $X$ runs over the channels of $R\otimes\Rbar$ (single channels $[\mu,\mu]$ and pairs $\{Z,Z'\}$).  The
coefficients $w_X(R)$ are rational functions of $A^2,q^2$.  They are \emph{not} fitted: they come from the universal
channel formula \eqref{eq:channel} below, and every one of the $319$ stored twist-knot polynomials with $|R|\le 6$
(29 diagrams $\times$ ten knots, plus the unknot) is a check, as are $H_R(4_1)$ for all $|R|=7$ and $20$ of the $22$
diagrams with $|R|=8$, and the symmetric data $[7],\dots,[10]$.  All $w_X(R)$ with $|R|\le6$ are tabulated exactly in
\LMPH{data/twist\_weights/twist\_weights\_le6.json}; the smallest non-rectangular cases are printed in Appendix~A.

\item \textbf{Universal structure (channel form).}  The $R$-dependence and the $m$-dependence separate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel}
H_R(\Tw_m)=1+\sum_{c\in\Chan(R)}F_c(R)\,\Phi_c(m),\qquad
F_c(R)=d_c\sum_X E_{cX}\,\psi_X(R),\qquad
\Phi_c(m)=\bigl(B^{\,m+1}\one\bigr)_c ,
\end{equation}
with an $R$-independent, knot-independent triangular ``twist matrix'' $B$ (Section~\ref{sec:B}).  Here $d_c$ and
$\psi_X(R)$ are closed (quantum dimensions and Koike characters), and $E$ is the universal matrix of the interpolation
formula of the repository (vanishing conditions).

\item \textbf{Closed: the single-channel sector, in one formula.}  Let $N:=\Et^{-1}$ (the ``binomial matrix'',
Section~\ref{sec:N}), so that $\Lam_c^{\,m}=\sum_X N_{cX}\,\Phi_X(m)$.  All rows of $N$ labelled by single channels
$[\lambda,\lambda]$ are given by one tableau formula,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tabsum}
N_{\lambda\mu}=\frac{(-A)^{-|\mu|}\,q^{-\kappa_\mu}}{\prod_{b\in\mu}[h_\mu(b)]}\;
\sum_{T}\;\prod_{\alpha\in\mu}\,[\lambda_{T(\alpha)}-c(\alpha)]\;D_{\lambda_{T(\alpha)}+c(\alpha)-2T(\alpha)-1},
\end{equation}
the sum running over the reverse tableaux $T$ of shape $\mu$ with entries in $\{0,\dots,\ell(\lambda)-1\}$ (the rows of
$\lambda$).  It contains the KNTZ twist matrix \eqref{eq:kntz} (the single rows of $B=\Et\Lam N$), the single rows of
$E$, and the product formula \eqref{eq:phiprod} for $\varphi_\lambda(-1)=N_{\lambda\lambda}$.  Verified exactly (over
$\mathbb Q(A,q)$) for all $841$ single entries with $|\lambda|\le6$, and modulo a large prime for every single entry
determined by the vanishing conditions up to nine boxes ($4096$ entries at $|\lambda|\le8$, $7396$ at $|\lambda|\le9$;
Section~\ref{sec:checks}).  For rectangular $R$ only single channels occur, so there the whole formula is closed
(the KNTZ result, in a different form).

\item \textbf{Closed: the top evolution coefficients, by one law.}  For every $R$ with $|R|\le 6$, $w_X(R)$ is a
product of $A^aq^b$, quantum numbers $[n]$ and factors $D_k$ exactly for the \emph{top} channels $X=[Z,Z']$ with
$Z\cup Z'=R$; all other coefficients are such a product times a polynomial in $A^2$ of degree $\le |R|-|Z\cup Z'|$
which does not factorise.  The top coefficients are all given by one formula (Section~\ref{sec:weights}),
\[
w_c(R)=m_c(R)\,d_c\,(-A)^{|R|}\,q^{\kappa_R}\prod_{b\in R}\frac{\{q^{h(b)}\}^2}{D_{c(b)}}\qquad(c\text{ top in }R),
\]
$d_c$ the quantum dimension of the composite(s) of $c$ and $m_c(R)=\langle s_{R/Z},s_{R/Z'}\rangle$ its multiplicity in
$R\otimes\Rbar$: the ratio $w_c(R)/(m_c(R)d_c)$ depends on $R$ only.  It holds for every channel whose only minimal
diagram is $R$ ($399$ channels of all kinds with a row up to ten boxes, two points; exact up to six boxes), all with
$m_c=1$, and at $R=U$ for all tops and co-tops of twelve diagrams with eight to ten boxes that contain a ghost
diagram; among them is $[4,3,2,1]$, where the first $m_c=2$ occurs, on its three separate/separate tops.  The minimal
diagram also fixes the constant term of the twist binomial, $\Phi_c(m)=\pi(R_c)+\sum_{X\ne\vac}
\Et_{cX}\Lam_X^m$ with $\pi(R)=\prod_{b\in R}Aq^{c(b)}/D_{c(b)}$, and $\rho=(-1)^{|R|}\pi\prod_b\{q^{h(b)}\}^2$ (vacuum law,
Observation~\ref{obs:vacuum}; all $380$ clean channels up to eleven boxes, two points).

\item \textbf{Closed: every one-cell pair channel, every column, every gap.}  Pair channels $\{Z,Z'\}$, $Z\ne Z'$,
exist precisely for non-rectangular $R$.  For a one-cell pair $c=\{Z,Z'\}$ with $V=Z\cap Z'$, $U=Z\cup Z'$
(Section~\ref{sec:pairs}): (i) every single column of the row $N_c$ is given by the \emph{four-row formula}
\eqref{eq:fourrow},
\[
2N_{c\mu}=N_{Z\mu}+N_{Z'\mu}+\{q^d\}^2\,\frac{N_{Z\mu}+N_{Z'\mu}-N_{U\mu}-N_{V\mu}}{D_{2c(b)}\,D_{2c(b')}},
\]
the average of the two single rows $Z$, $Z'$ plus a divided second difference of the closed single rows over the
square $V\subset Z,Z'\subset U$ (at a resonant column it gives the regular combination $N_{c\mu}+\sum_{U_X=\mu}N_{cX}$);
(ii) every pair column $N_{cX}$ (hence $B_{cX}$, $E_{cX}$), at \emph{any} gap $|U|-|U_X|$, is given by the
\emph{root formula} \eqref{eq:roots}: the chain expansion of $N_{cX}$ must be regular at the roots of
$\Lam_c=\Lam_X$ that are not zeros of a $D_k$, which fixes the pure-$q$ factor $T_{cX}$ of $B_{cX}$ as an explicit
sum over chains of adjacent steps evaluated at those roots; the adjacent steps are the residue formula
\eqref{eq:adjres} (a product), gap two is the $q$-symmetrised composition \eqref{eq:gap2}; (iii) the single columns of
$B$ follow from $NB=\Lam N$ \eqref{eq:Bsingle}, and outside the resonant columns are a closed combination of the three
KNTZ rows of $U$, $Z$, $Z'$ (union formula \eqref{eq:union}).  Consequently every entry of $N$, $\Et$, $E$, $B$ is
closed for every channel that is a single or a one-cell pair, and the twist-knot formula is completely closed for every
$R$ whose channels are of these two kinds: all $|R|\le5$, $8$ of the $11$ diagrams with six boxes, $11$ of $15$ with
seven, $11$ of $22$ with eight, all hooks $[r,1]$, $[2,1^{r-1}]$ and all rectangles.  Verified by the script up to
seven boxes (all $34$ one-cell pairs, gaps one to four) and in the analysis modulo $p$ up to ten boxes (gaps one to
seven, $2629$ single and $1176$ pair columns at ten boxes alone), without a single mismatch; nothing is fitted.  End to
end, $H_R(\Tw_m)$ built from these closed rows alone (no vanishing condition solved) agrees with every stored polynomial
and with Rosso--Jones for all $76$ such diagrams up to ten boxes (check \LMPH{closed}), and with Rosso--Jones for the $72$
such diagrams with $11$ to $14$ boxes (analysis).  The root average has a per-entry form for most columns: the pure-$q$
factor $T_{cX}$ is a product of two Okounkov binomial coefficients at zonal parameters for every column $X$ of a certain
``axis'' type, which includes all $X$ with $|U_X|\le5$ (Observation~\ref{obs:zonal}).

\item \textbf{One kernel for every pair row.}  All the pair rows of $N$ on single columns, for one-cell and
multi-cell pairs alike, come from one sign-flip kernel \eqref{eq:kernel} applied to the closed single rows: a sum over
the sign patterns of the moved cells of $c$, each state weighted by a product of brackets $\{\sqrt{u_y/u_x}\}$ between
the two sides, $1/\{u_e\}$ per cell, and a domino factor within a side.  For one-cell pairs it is the four-row formula;
for the two-cell pairs (first at six boxes: $\{[2,2],[4]\}$ in $[4,2]$, $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$ in $[3,2,1]$,
$\{[1^4],[2,2]\}$ in $[2,2,1,1]$) it is verified on every row with data up to ten boxes that is not resonant
(domino/domino and domino/separate, $4\,166$ columns, no failure), and for the line pairs (both sides a row, or both a
column, of $n$ cells) on every state in which one side has at most one of its cells present or at most one absent, for
every $n$.  The rest of a
non-resonant two-cell row is closed as follows (Observation~\ref{obs:twocell}): the diagonal $N_{cc}$ and the first pair
column are products for every pair with at most two moved cells per side; the pair columns of the weight of $c$ (two or
four ``partners'' and one more column) are products; the columns of larger weight vanish; and the columns of smaller
weight, which are not products in general, are Lagrange interpolants: on the channels with the same domino orientation,
together with the singles and one-cell pairs, each such column is the polynomial in the composite power sums that
vanishes on the lighter channels and takes the closed top values, an explicit ratio of two determinants (equivalently,
the unique solution of a filtration plus a zero pattern).  The resonant two-cell pairs (a vertical against a horizontal domino,
eigenvalue identical to that of a single) are closed in the same way: their single columns live partly outside $U$, on
$[V,R_{\min}]$, and are given by an extended kernel there; their light columns are Lagrange interpolants on a space
enlarged by the lower resonant and cross-oriented two-cell columns, whose coordinates are closed products, and three
heavy columns are products (Observation~\ref{obs:resrows}).  Both filtrations are cases of one rule that holds for every
row: $\Et_c$ annihilates the even composite power-sum polynomials of weight $<|R_{\min}(c)|-n_c$, $n_c$ the number of
moved cells per side.  From weight $10$ on the power sums satisfy relations on the channels (the first is
$\bar E_1s_{333}$), and (L) holds on their restriction, with exactly the right number of nodes up to weight $14$.  Some
columns are closed outright: the hook pair columns and the one-row and one-column singles are $\mathrm{Alt}$ and
$\mathrm{Sym}$ products of windowed elementary and complete functions of the composite particles, on both families, and
the weight-four two-cell columns and the weight-ten relation are super Schur functions of the moved cells, $12s_{22}$
and $\bar E_1s_{333}$ (Observation~\ref{obs:interp}).  The
diagonal of every three-cell pair with one effective shape, and of every pair with a line side, is one hook product over
$V$ with hinge shifts, and a line row has as heavy pair columns exactly its chain pairs, the two nearest of them closed
products for every $n$ (Observation~\ref{obs:threecell}).
\textbf{Not closed.}  (i) A combinatorial formula for the other columns of the Lagrange basis (first the one-cell
columns not of axis type), and the three-cell block of the three-cell line rows.  (ii) The deep states of the line pairs with $n\ge6$ (first at $18$ boxes); for rows with $n\ge3$ moved cells per
side, the heavy pair columns other than the line partners, the pair columns of the rows that are not line rows, half of
the kernel states of a domino plus a separate cell against a line, bent sides beyond the diagonal, and the anomalous
pairs ($m_c(U)=0$); separate/separate two-cell pairs (no data).

\item \textbf{Ghosts: where the channel form stops, and how the formula continues.}  Up to seven boxes the vanishing
conditions determine every row of $E$ once enough nodes are used; the rank deficiencies seen at seven and eight boxes with
the engine's default node bound are an artefact of that bound, with one exception: the five top channels of $R=[4,3,1]$
and the five of $[3,2,2,1]$ (eight boxes) keep a one-dimensional common kernel for every node bound, and no choice of the
free parameter makes \eqref{eq:channel} reproduce $H_R(\Tw_m)$ for these two $R$ (with the default gauge already
$H_R(4_1)$ is wrong).  The kernel is a \emph{ghost}: a combination of characters that vanishes on every $R$ not containing
$U=[4,3,1]$ and belongs to no channel.  It adds one term of the same three-factor form to \eqref{eq:Usum}, whose constant
$Z_g$ is fixed by the trefoil (Rosso--Jones at $m=1$, $R=U$); nothing is fitted.  With this term the formula agrees with
the strengthened Formula II of the other branch for all $137$ diagrams up to ten boxes except $[4,3,2,1]$ ($m=-4,\dots,4$,
two points), in particular with the stored $H_R(4_1)$ of $[4,3,1]$ and $[3,2,2,1]$ (Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}).  Which
diagrams carry ghosts is a combinatorial rule checked up to $18$ boxes: never for at most two corners, always for four or
more, and by the Kerov gaps for three corners.  Their number has an exact form, one super-Cauchy identity per corner, and
is closed for three rows with a gap at most two or $s_3=3$; for three corners the leading part of the ghost is closed, the
composite square of a cross product of the corner contents.  Open: diagrams with two or more ghosts (first $[6,4,1]$ and $[5,4,2]$ at
eleven boxes) and the four-corner diagrams ($[4,3,2,1]$, ten ghosts, at ten boxes).  For these the tops are still fixed,
by the top-weight law with its multiplicities, so at $R=U$ exactly $\mathrm{ex}(U)$ numbers are open (ten for
$[4,3,2,1]$, where everything else agrees with the closed block of the other branch).  At every colour $R\supseteq U$ the
twist knots see the layer of $U$ through one symmetric form, $M(U)/\rho(U)$ times the double-braid block $K_U$ at $R=U$
(from the ideal identity; from the closed block of $[4,3,2,1]$ at $R=U$ alone this reproduces the other branch's
eleven-box twist knots, $33/33$).  But polynomiality in $A$, the amphichirality of $4_1$, Rosso--Jones, and the double
braids at $R=U$ with their mirror symmetry do not fix $K_U$ from $\mathrm{ex}(U)=2$ on, and a closed $H_R(4_1)$ would add
nothing to Rosso--Jones.  What fixes the twist-knot content of every layer is the regularity law (Observation~\ref{obs:law}): with
everything above $[4,3,1]$, $[3,2,2,1]$ unknown it is the final form above.

\item \textbf{Beyond twist knots: double braids and Borromean rings.}  The same constants give every antiparallel
double braid and the Borromean rings (Section~\ref{sec:cubic}).  On every vanishing ideal the twist binomial at $m=1$
is a monomial, $\Phi_c(1)=M(R_c)=(-1)^{|R_c|}A^{2|R_c|}q^{2\kappa(R_c)}$ (monomial law, the second half of the ideal
identity), and
\begin{gather*}
H_R(\mathrm{DB}(m,n))=\sum_c\frac{Z_c}{M(R_c)}\,\cf Rc\,\Phi_c(m)\Phi_c(n),\\
b(R_1,R_2,R_3)=1+\sum_cZ_c\frac{\rho(R_c)^2}{M(R_c)}\,\cf{R_1}c\cf{R_2}c\cf{R_3}c .
\end{gather*}
Both are contractions of one symmetric cubic tensor.  At nine and ten boxes ten ``deferred'' diagrams add terms of the
same shape with closed product constants; up to ten boxes the twist knots do not see them.  Checked exactly against the
$18$ stored knots that are double braids (all $R$ with $|R|\le6$) and the Borromean rings $L6a4$ (all $29$ colours up to
six boxes), and modulo $p$ against the strengthened engine of the other branch up to ten boxes, except $R\supseteq[4,3,2,1]$.
\end{itemize}

Nothing in this note is proved.  The statements are conjectures checked on the data described in
Section~\ref{sec:checks}; \LMPH{scripts/twist\_formula\_check.py} re-runs every check from the data files.

\section{The formula in one place}\label{sec:onepage}

This section states the conjecture in its final form, with every ingredient and the data it is checked on.  The later
sections derive the pieces and record how they were found.  Nothing here is proved.

\paragraph{Ingredients.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Numbers.}  For a cell $b=(i,j)$ of a diagram $R$: the content $c(b)=j-i$, the hook length $h(b)$, and
$\kappa_R=\sum_bc(b)$.  Further $[n]=(q^n-q^{-n})/(q-q^{-1})$, $\{x\}=x-x^{-1}$, $D_k=Aq^k-A^{-1}q^{-k}$, and
\[
\rho(R)=(-A)^{|R|}\,q^{\kappa_R}\prod_{b\in R}\frac{\{q^{h(b)}\}^2}{D_{c(b)}},\qquad M(R)=(-1)^{|R|}A^{2|R|}q^{2\kappa_R}.
\]
\item \emph{Channels.}  A channel is a composite $X=[Z,Z']$ with $|Z|=|Z'|$, taken up to $[Z,Z']\sim[Z',Z]$: a single
$[\mu]=[\mu,\mu]$ or a pair $\{Z,Z'\}$.  It carries the eigenvalue $\Lam_X=A^{2|Z|}q^{2(\kappa_Z+\kappa_{Z'})}$, the
quantum dimension $d_X$ of its composite(s), and the normalised Koike character $\chih_X(R)$ at the colour $R$;
$\psi_X=\chih_X-1$.  $X$ is \emph{present} at $R$ if $m_X(R)=\langle s_{R/Z},s_{R/Z'}\rangle>0$, and $\Chan(R)$ is the set
of channels present at $R$.  A minimal diagram of $X$ is a minimal $R$ at which $X$ is present, and $M_X(R)$ denotes
the smallest minimal diagram of $X$ contained in $R$.
\item \emph{Ideals and rows.}  For a diagram $U$, $I_U$ is the space of $f=\sum_{X\in\Chan(U)}f_X\psi_X$ with $f(S)=0$
for every diagram $S\not\supseteq U$; here $f(R)=\sum_Xf_X\psi_X(R)$ and $\varphi_f(m)=\sum_Xf_X(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1)$.  The
\emph{row} of a channel $c$ at one of its minimal diagrams $M$ is the $e\in I_M$ with $e_c=1$, $e=0$ on the other tops
and co-tops of $M$, and $e(S)=0$ at every $S\supseteq M$ at which $c$ is absent.  Its \emph{term} at $R\supseteq M$ is
the vector
\[
\tau_{c,M}(R)=d_c\,\frac{e(R)}{\varphi_e(-1)}\;e\;=\;Z_c\,\cf Rc\,\Et_c
\]
in the notation of Section~\ref{sec:universal} ($Z_c=d_cN_{cc}\rho(M)$, $\cf Rc$ the colour binomial, $\Et_c=e/\varphi_e(-1)$
the row of $\Et=N^{-1}$).
\item \emph{Below the ghosts.}  Every diagram $U$ with ghosts, $\mathrm{ex}(U)=\dim I_U-\#\{\text{tops}\}
-\#\{\text{co-tops}\}\ge1$, contains $[4,3,1]$ or $[3,2,2,1]$ (Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}; all $376$ ghost diagrams of the
census of $819$ diagrams up to $18$ boxes).  We say that $M$ is \emph{below the ghosts} if it contains neither.  There the
row of every channel is unique, and Table~\ref{tab:N} gives closed formulas for it; where none is known, the row is the
unique solution of the vanishing conditions, a finite linear system in the closed characters.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The regularity law.}  Write $H_R(\Tw_m)=1+\sum_Xw_X(R)\,(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1)$.  For every $R$ and every channel $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
w_X(R)=0\quad\text{if }X\notin\Chan(R),\qquad
\frac{w_X(R)}{d_X\,\rho(R)}\in\mathbb Q(q)[A^2]\quad\text{of degree}\le|R|-|M_X(R)|.
\end{equation}
At the tops of $R$ ($M_X(R)=R$) the quotient is a constant, and \eqref{eq:law} contains the top-weight law
\eqref{eq:topweight}.  In general it sharpens Observation~\ref{obs:top}: the product is $d_X\rho(R)$ for every channel, and
the degree bound uses $M_X(R)$ instead of $Z\cup Z'$ ($|M_X(R)|\ge|Z\cup Z'|$).  The checks are in
Observation~\ref{obs:law}.  On the stored exact weights the bound is attained on every label where it is positive.

\begin{conjecture}[the universal twist-knot formula]\label{conj:final}
For every diagram $R$ and every $m\in\mathbb Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final}
\boxed{\;H_R(\Tw_m)=1+\sum_Xw_X(R)\bigl(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1\bigr),\qquad
w(R)=\sum_{\substack{(c,M):\ M\subseteq R\\ M\not\supseteq[4,3,1],\,[3,2,2,1]}}\tau_{c,M}(R)\;+\;t(R),\;}
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the channels $c$ of $R$ and those of their minimal diagrams $M\subseteq R$ that are below the
ghosts, and $t(R)$ is the unique element of
\[
V_R=\sum_{\substack{U\subseteq R\\ U\supseteq[4,3,1]\ \text{or}\ U\supseteq[3,2,2,1]}}I_U
\]
for which $w(R)$ obeys the regularity law \eqref{eq:law}.
\end{conjecture}

Every ingredient is explicit: the terms below the ghosts are the closed channel terms of Sections
\ref{sec:single}--\ref{sec:universal}, and $t(R)$ is the solution of a finite linear system with closed coefficients.
At fixed $q$, take $N$ values $A_1,\dots,A_N$; the unknowns are the coordinates of $t(R)$ in a basis of $V_R$ at each
$A_i$, and every label $X$ gives $N-g_X-1$ equations, the divided differences of order $g_X+1$ of
$w_X(R)/(d_X\rho(R))$ in $A^2$ ($g_X=|R|-|M_X(R)|$; for an absent channel $w_X(R)=0$ at every point).  In every case
tried the solution is unique ($N=24$ and $N=36$).  Remarks:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] \emph{Below the ghosts} ($R\not\supseteq[4,3,1],[3,2,2,1]$, e.g.\ every $R$ with at most seven boxes) $V_R=0$, and
\eqref{eq:final} is the channel formula, $H_R(\Tw_m)=\sum_cZ_c\cf Rc\Phi_c(m)$, term by term explicit.  (The rows must be
the unique ones: with the vanishing conditions cut off too early, at $[4,2,1]$ and $[3,2,1,1]$, the rows are not
determined, and a wrong choice is caught by the law; Observation~\ref{obs:law}.)  Above them the law
replaces three things that were separate rules before: the gauge of the rows of the tops of ghost diagrams (the zero
rule), the ghost terms with the trefoil rule for $Z_g$ \eqref{eq:ghost}, and the open layers of the diagrams with
$\mathrm{ex}(U)\ge2$ and of $[4,3,2,1]$.  All of these now come out of one solve, with no trefoil, Rosso--Jones, top-law or
Formula~I input.  Also the deferred terms of Section~\ref{sec:cubic} lie in $V_R$ and come out.
\item[(b)] \emph{What comes out.}  At $R=U$ the multiplicity top law $w_c(U)=m_c(U)\,d_c\,\rho(U)$ (with $m_c=2$ on the three
separate/separate tops of $[4,3,2,1]$), the trefoil-rule constants $Z_g$ of the excess-one diagrams (including the
conjectured family $Z_{[a,3,1]}$ of Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}), and the closed $[4,3,2,1]$ block of the other branch
label by label.  So the closed block is exactly the regular completion at $R=U$, its twist-invisible coordinates
included.
\item[(c)] \emph{What it does not say.}  The law fixes the total $t(R)$, which is all that the twist knots at $R$ need.  It
does not fix the split of $t(R)$ into the layers $G^U$ of the diagrams $U\subseteq R$ as symmetric forms; the double braids
and the Borromean rings need more of the cubic tensor (Section~\ref{sec:cubic}).
\item[(d)] \emph{The input cannot be made smaller in this way.}  With only the single channels known (the closed KNTZ
sector) and every other top of every $U\subseteq R$ unknown, the law leaves exactly one free direction at $R=[3,1]$; with the
singles and the one-cell pairs known, it leaves exactly one at $[5,2,1]$.  The top-weight law adds nothing here, since it
holds on the whole ideal (Observation~\ref{obs:monomial}).  So the channels below the ghosts are input, and the law is a
statement about what lies above them.
\item[(e)] \emph{The most explicit part.}  For a single channel with $s_{R/\lambda}=s_W$ (multiplicity one; for instance
every $\lambda\subseteq R$ when $R$ is a rectangle), the term is a product and the twist binomial one determinant
(Observation~\ref{obs:bc}):
\[
Z_\lambda\cf R\lambda=d_\lambda\,\{q\}^{2|\lambda|}\,\frac{H_q(\lambda)\,H_q(R)}{H_q(W)}
\prod_{b\in R}\frac{D_{\delta_\lambda(b)}}{D_{c(b)}},
\]
with $\delta_\lambda(i,j)=(\lambda_i-i)-(\lambda'_j-j)$ and $H_q(X)=\prod_{b\in X}[h(b)]$,
and $\Phi_\lambda(m)$ is the $n\times n$ determinant \eqref{eq:Phidet}.  For a rectangle this is the KNTZ formula; for a pair
with one minimal diagram and $s_{R/Z}=s_{R/Z'}=s_W$, $\cf Rc=\cf RZ\cf R{Z'}/(\cf{R_c}Z\cf{R_c}{Z'})$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conventions, data, and the twist parameter}\label{sec:conv}

Standard convention of the repository (\LMPH{docs/CONVENTIONS.md}): $H_R$ is reduced ($H_R(\text{unknot})=1$),
topologically framed, $H_{R^T}(A,q)=H_R(A,-1/q)$, mirror $H_R(K^*;A,q)=H_R(K;1/A,1/q)$; the positive trefoil has
$H_{[1]}=A^2q^2+A^2q^{-2}-A^4$.  Throughout
\[
D_k=Aq^k-A^{-1}q^{-k},\qquad [n]=\frac{q^n-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad \{x\}=x-x^{-1},\qquad
\kappa_\lambda=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(j-i),
\]
and for a box $b=(i,j)$ of a diagram, $c(b)=j-i$ is its content, $a(b)$ its arm and $l(b)$ its leg length,
$h(b)=a(b)+l(b)+1$ its hook length.  We also write $x=A^2$, $y=q^2$; then $A q^k D_k = xy^k-1$, $Aq^kD_{-k}=x-y^k$,
$q\{q\}=y-1$, $q[2]=y+1$, $q^2[3]=y^2+y+1$.

\paragraph{Twist knots.}  $\Tw_m:=\mathrm{DoubleBraid}(m,1)$ in the notation of \LMPH{homfly.knots.families}: the
antiparallel double braid with $m$ full twists in one region and one in the other; $\mathrm{DoubleBraid}(1,1)=3_1$,
$\mathrm{DoubleBraid}(1,-1)=4_1$, $\Tw_0=$ unknot.  The identification with the stored knots (checked on the fundamental
polynomials against \LMPH{data/homfly}) is

\section{The evolution formula}\label{sec:evol}

$\Tw_m$ is obtained from $\Tw_0$ by inserting $m$ full twists into a two-strand antiparallel braid, so by the
standard eigenvalue argument $H_R(\Tw_m)$ is a linear combination of $m$-th powers of the eigenvalues of the full twist
on the irreducible composite representations $Q=[Z,Z']\subset R\otimes\Rbar$ ($Z,Z'\subseteq R$, $|Z|=|Z'|$),
\[
\lambda_{[Z,Z']}=A^{2|Z|}q^{\,2(\kappa_Z+\kappa_{Z'})}
\]
in the normalisation in which the unknot ($m=0$) gives $1$.  The conjugate composites $[Z,Z']$ and $[Z',Z]$ have the
same eigenvalue and the same dimension and are treated together: a \emph{channel} is either a single $[\mu,\mu]$ or a
pair $\{Z,Z'\}$ with $Z\ne Z'$.  A channel $c$ is present in $R$ when its multiplicity
$m_c(R)=\sum_\gamma c^R_{Z\gamma}c^R_{Z'\gamma}$ (Littlewood--Richardson coefficients) is non-zero; $\Chan(R)$ is the set
of present channels.  Pairs are present only for non-rectangular $R$: for rectangular $R$ the product $R\otimes\Rbar$
is multiplicity free and contains only $[\mu,\mu]$.  This gives \eqref{eq:evol} with the vacuum contribution written
as $1-\sum_X w_X(R)$.

Two remarks on what the data alone can do.  (i) With eleven twist knots ($m=-5,\dots,5$) the coefficients of
\eqref{eq:evol} can be solved from the data only when the number of distinct eigenvalues is at most eleven; this
holds for all $|R|\le4$ and for $[5],[4,1],[3,2],[2,2,1],[2,1,1,1],[1^5],[6],[3,3],[2,2,2],[1^6]$ but fails for
$[3,1,1]$ (13 eigenvalues), $[5,1]$ (14), $[2,1^4]$ (14), $[4,2]$ (17), $[2,2,1,1]$ (17), $[4,1,1]$ (18), $[3,1,1,1]$
(18) and $[3,2,1]$ (20).  Where the data suffice, the solution is unique (Vandermonde), hence equals the tabulated
$w_X(R)$ since those reproduce all eleven values.  (ii) Resonances: distinct channels can share an eigenvalue.  Up to
six boxes this happens only in $[3,2,1]$, where
\[
\Lam_{[2,1]}=\Lam_{\{[1,1,1],[3]\}}=A^6,\qquad \Lam_{[2,2]}=\Lam_{\{[2,1,1],[3,1]\}}=A^8,\qquad
\Lam_{[3,1,1]}=\Lam_{\{[2,2,1],[3,2]\}}=A^{10};
\]
twist knots then determine only the sum of the two weights, while the channel formula gives each of them.

\section{The channel formula and the twist matrix}\label{sec:B}

The repository's interpolation formula for antiparallel double braids (\LMPH{src/homfly/methods/interpolation.py},
``An interpolation formula for colored HOMFLY polynomials of the figure-eight knot and of double-braid knots in
arbitrary representations'') reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F2}
H_R(m,n)=1+\sum_{c\in\Chan(R)}F_c(R)\,\frac{\varphi_c(m)\varphi_c(n)}{\varphi_c(1)\varphi_c(-1)},\qquad
F_c(R)=d_c\sum_{X}E_{cX}\,\psi_X(R),\qquad \varphi_c(m)=\sum_X E_{cX}\bigl(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $d_c$ is the quantum dimension of the composite(s) of $c$, $\psi_X(R)=\hat\chi_X(R)-1$ is the normalised Koike
character of $X$ evaluated at $q^{2(R+\rho)}$ (a closed function of $R$: Schur functions of the power sums
$p_k=(1-A^{-2k})/(1-q^{-2k})+(q^{2k}-1)\sum_{b\in R}q^{2kc(b)}$), and the row $E_c$ is universal: $E_{cc}=1$ and
$\sum_X E_{cX}\psi_X(\nu)=0$ for every $\nu$ in which $c$ is absent, $X$ running over the channels $X<c$ present in the
minimal diagrams of $c$.  $E$ depends neither on $R$ nor on the knot.

Twist knots are the case $n=1$, and \eqref{eq:F2} becomes \eqref{eq:channel} with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
\Phi_c(m)=\frac{\varphi_c(m)}{\varphi_c(-1)}=\sum_{X}\Et_{cX}\bigl(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1\bigr),\qquad
\Et_{cX}=\frac{E_{cX}}{\varphi_c(-1)},\qquad w_X(R)=\sum_c F_c(R)\,\Et_{cX}.
\end{equation}
Append the vacuum: $\Lam_\vac=1$, $\Et_{\vac\vac}=1$, $\Et_{c\vac}=-\sum_{X\ne\vac}\Et_{cX}$, so that
$\Phi(m)=\Et\,\Lam^m\one$ with $\Phi_\vac\equiv1$ and $\Phi_c(-1)=1$ for all $c$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bdef}
\Phi(m+1)=B\,\Phi(m),\qquad B:=\Et\,\Lam\,\Et^{-1},\qquad \Phi(-1)=\one,\qquad\text{so}\quad \Phi_c(m)=(B^{m+1}\one)_c .
\end{equation}
$B$ is lower triangular with respect to the order $X<c$ (its diagonal is $\Lam_c$), $B_{\vac X}=\delta_{X\vac}$, and
every other row sums to zero ($B\one=\Phi(0)=e_\vac$).  The columns of $\Et$ are the eigenvectors of $B$: whenever
$B$ is known, $\Et$ (hence $E$ and $\varphi_c(-1)$) follows from the recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigrec}
\Et_{cX}=\frac{\sum_{X<Y<c}B_{cY}\,\Et_{YX}}{\Lam_X-\Lam_c}\quad(X\ne c),\qquad
\Et_{cc}=\frac{1-\sum_{X\ne c,\vac}\Et_{cX}(\Lam_X^{-1}-1)}{\Lam_c^{-1}-1},\qquad
E_{cX}=\frac{\Et_{cX}}{\Et_{cc}},\quad \varphi_c(-1)=\frac1{\Et_{cc}}.
\end{equation}
So the content of the twist-knot formula is the matrix $B$: $\Lam$, $d_c$ and $\psi_X(R)$ are closed, and
\eqref{eq:channel}--\eqref{eq:Bdef} express $H_R(\Tw_m)$ for all $m$ through $B$.  Explicit in $m$: expanding
$B^{m+1}\one$ in eigenvalues gives back \eqref{eq:evol}--\eqref{eq:Phi}.

\begin{conjecture}[twist-knot formula]\label{conj:main}
For every diagram $R$ and every $m\in\mathbb Z$, $H_R(\Tw_m)=1+\sum_{c\in\Chan(R)}F_c(R)\,(B^{m+1}\one)_c$ with
$F_c(R)$, $B$ as above, the single rows of $B$ given by \eqref{eq:kntz} and the pair rows by the vanishing conditions.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The single sector is closed}\label{sec:single}

A single row of $B$ has non-zero entries only at single columns $\mu\subseteq\lambda$ (no pair entries; this holds for
every single channel with $|\lambda|\le 8$ whose vanishing system has full rank, while the engine's gauge rows of the
seven rank-deficient singles listed below do contain pair entries, one way to see that this gauge is not the right
one), and

\begin{conjecture}[KNTZ identity]\label{conj:kntz}
For single channels $\lambda,\mu$ ($\mu\subseteq\lambda$)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kntz}
B_{\lambda\mu}=(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}\;\Lam_\lambda\;
\frac{s_{\lambda'/\mu'}(p^\circ)\,s_{\mu}(p^\circ)}{s_{\lambda}(p^\circ)},\qquad
\Lam_\lambda=A^{2|\lambda|}q^{4\kappa_\lambda},\qquad
s_\nu(p^\circ)=\frac{q^{\kappa_\nu}\{q\}^{|\nu|}}{\prod_{b\in\nu}\{q^{h(b)}\}},
\end{equation}
where $s_{\lambda'/\mu'}$ is the skew Schur function at the principal specialisation $p^\circ_k=\{q\}^k/\{q^k\}$
($\lambda'$ is the transposed diagram).  This is the twist matrix of Kameyama--Nawata--Tao--Zhang (``Cyclotomic
expansions of HOMFLY-PT colored by rectangular Young diagrams'', arXiv:1902.02275), rewritten in the conventions of
Section~\ref{sec:conv}.
\end{conjecture}

Examples: $B_{\lambda\vac}=(-1)^{|\lambda|}A^{2|\lambda|}q^{2\kappa_\lambda}$ (since $s_{\lambda'}/s_\lambda=q^{-2\kappa_\lambda}$);
the rows $[2,1]$, $[2,1,1]$, $[3,1]$ are printed in Appendix~A.2.  Consequences:

\begin{fact}\label{fact:phi}
$\varphi_\lambda(-1)$ is a product:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phiprod}
\varphi_\lambda(-1)=(-A)^{-|\lambda|}\,q^{-\kappa_\lambda}\prod_{b\in\lambda}D_{\,a(b)-l(b)+2c(b)} ,
\end{equation}
e.g.\ $\varphi_{[2]}(-1)=A^{-2}q^{-1}D_1D_2$, $\varphi_{[1,1]}(-1)=A^{-2}qD_{-1}D_{-2}$,
$\varphi_{[2,1]}(-1)=-A^{-3}D_{-2}D_{0}D_{2}$.
\end{fact}

\begin{fact}\label{fact:E}
The single rows of $E$ follow from \eqref{eq:kntz} by \eqref{eq:eigrec}.  They are products of $D_k$'s and quantum
numbers (as in Appendix~B of the interpolation paper, e.g.\ $E_{[2],[1]}=-[2]D_1/D_0$, $E_{[3],[2]}=-[3]D_3/D_1$),
with one systematic exception: the entry $E_{\lambda,[1]}$ is not a product for the non-hook, non-rectangular
$\lambda=[2,2,1],[3,2],[2,2,1,1],[3,2,1],[4,2]$ (it is for hooks and rectangles).
\end{fact}

\paragraph{Status.}  Exact identity of \eqref{eq:kntz} with the vanishing-condition rows for all $29$ singles with
$|\lambda|\le 6$ (over $\mathbb Q(A^2,q^2)$); identity of the rows derived by \eqref{eq:eigrec} with the engine's rows
modulo $p=10^9+7$ at random points for all $59$ singles with $|\lambda|\le8$ whose vanishing system has full rank
(\LMPH{check kntz}).  For the seven singles $[4,2,1]$, $[3,2,1,1]$, $[5,2,1]$, $[4,2,1,1]$, $[3,2,2,1]$, $[4,3,1]$,
$[3,2,1,1,1]$ the vanishing system is rank-deficient (by one or two); there the engine's gauge row and the KNTZ row
differ, $\varphi_\lambda(-1)$ nevertheless agrees, and both choices give the same $H_R(4_1)$ wherever the formula
works at all ($|R|=7$: $15/15$; $|R|=8$: $20/22$ either way).  The KNTZ row is the canonical choice for these
channels.  (With the vanishing conditions imposed on all diagrams with at most $13$ boxes the deficiency disappears
for all of them except $[4,3,1]$, $[3,2,2,1]$, and the determined rows agree with KNTZ; see Section~\ref{sec:checks}.)

\section{The binomial matrix and the closed single sector}\label{sec:N}

The matrix $\Et$ of \eqref{eq:Phi} expands the ``exponentials'' $\Lam_X^m$ into the basis $\Phi_c(m)$; its inverse does
the opposite.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ndef}
N:=\Et^{-1}\quad\text{on }\{\vac\}\cup\Chan,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \Lam_c^{\,m}=\sum_{X\le c}N_{cX}\,\Phi_X(m)\quad(m\in\mathbb Z),
\end{equation}
the ``binomial expansion'' of $\Lam_c^m$ (for $\Phi_X(m)$ is a polynomial-like basis: $\Phi_X(-1)=1$, $\Phi_X(0)=0$,
$\Phi_\vac\equiv1$).  Since $\Et$ is unitriangular up to its diagonal, $N$ is triangular, $N_{cc}=1/\Et_{cc}=\varphi_c(-1)$,
and $B=\Et\Lam N$.  Setting $m=-1$ in \eqref{eq:Ndef} gives $\Lam_c^{-1}=\sum_X N_{cX}$, setting $m=0$ gives
$N_{c\vac}=1$ for every channel $c$.  The single rows of $N$ have no pair entries (all $|\lambda|\le 9$).  $N$ is the
object with the simplest closed form:

\begin{conjecture}[tableau formula for the single sector]\label{conj:tab}
For single channels $\lambda$, $\mu$ and $\ell=\ell(\lambda)$ the number of rows of $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tab}
N_{\lambda\mu}=\frac{(-A)^{-|\mu|}\,q^{-\kappa_\mu}}{\prod_{b\in\mu}[h_\mu(b)]}\;P_\mu(\lambda),\qquad
P_\mu(\lambda)=\sum_{T}\;\prod_{\alpha\in\mu}w\bigl(T(\alpha),\alpha\bigr),\qquad
w(i,\alpha)=[\lambda_i-c(\alpha)]\;D_{\lambda_i+c(\alpha)-2i-1},
\end{equation}
where $T$ runs over the reverse tableaux of shape $\mu$ with entries in $\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}$ (weakly decreasing along
rows, strictly decreasing down columns), $i=T(\alpha)$ is a $0$-based row index of $\lambda$, $\lambda_i$ the length
of that row, and $c(\alpha)$ the content of the box $\alpha$ of $\mu$.
\end{conjecture}

Equivalent forms of the weight: $w(i,\alpha)=\sum_{j=c(\alpha)}^{\lambda_i-1}D_{2(j-i)}$, the sum of $D_{2c(b)}$ over the
boxes $b=(i,j)$ of row $i$ of $\lambda$ from column $c(\alpha)$ to the end of the row (the row is continued to the left
by boxes of negative column index when $c(\alpha)<0$, and the sum is $-\sum_{j=\lambda_i}^{c(\alpha)-1}$ when
$c(\alpha)>\lambda_i$); and
\[
w(i,\alpha)=\frac{(Z_i+Z_i^{-1})-(\zeta_{i\alpha}+\zeta_{i\alpha}^{-1})}{\{q\}},\qquad
Z_i=Aq^{2\lambda_i-2i-1},\quad \zeta_{i\alpha}=Aq^{2c(\alpha)-2i-1},
\]
i.e.\ $\zeta_{i\alpha}$ is $Z_i$ with $\lambda_i$ replaced by $c(\alpha)$.  In this form $P_\mu$ is a tableau sum of
interpolation type in the variables $Z_i=q^{2(\lambda_i+\rho_i)}$ (with $A=q^N$ absorbed into $\rho$), of the kind
appearing in the combinatorial formulas for Okounkov's $BC$-type interpolation polynomials at $q=t$ and, in the
classical limit, for the shifted Schur functions of Okounkov--Olshanski; we did not use this identification, it is noted
for orientation only.  Consequences and special cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item $P_\mu(\lambda)=0$ unless $\mu\subseteq\lambda$ (the interpolation property; each weight $w(i,\alpha)$
vanishes when $\lambda_i=c(\alpha)$), and $N_{\lambda\lambda}=\varphi_\lambda(-1)$ reproduces the product
\eqref{eq:phiprod}: for $\mu=\lambda$ a single tableau survives, e.g.\ for $\lambda=[2,1]$ the tableau
$T=\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&\end{smallmatrix}$ with $P_{[2,1]}([2,1])=[3]D_{-2}D_0D_2$.
\item First column: $N_{\lambda,[1]}=-\frac1A\sum_{b\in\lambda}D_{2c(b)}$ (the ``content sum'' of $\lambda$);
$\mu=[2]$ and $\mu=[1,1]$:
\[
N_{\lambda,[2]}=\frac{1}{A^2q[2]}\sum_{i\ge i'}[\lambda_i]D_{\lambda_i-2i-1}\,[\lambda_{i'}-1]D_{\lambda_{i'}-2i'},\qquad
N_{\lambda,[1,1]}=\frac{q}{A^2[2]}\sum_{i>i'}[\lambda_i]D_{\lambda_i-2i-1}\,[\lambda_{i'}+1]D_{\lambda_{i'}-2i'-2}.
\]
\item Row $[2,1]$: $N_{[2,1],[1]}=-[3]D_0/A$, $N_{[2,1],[2]}=[3]D_0D_2/(A^2q[2])$, $N_{[2,1],[1,1]}=q[3]D_{-2}D_0/(A^2[2])$,
$N_{[2,1],[2,1]}=-D_{-2}D_0D_2/A^3$.
\item For a hook $\lambda$ the sum collapses to a product,
$N_{\lambda\mu}=(-A)^{-|\mu|}q^{-\kappa_\mu}\prod_{b\in\mu}\frac{[h_\lambda(b)]}{[h_\mu(b)]}D_{a_\lambda(b)-l_\lambda(b)+2c(b)}$
(arms, legs and hooks measured in $\lambda$); for non-hook $\lambda$ this product is wrong already at
$N_{[2,2],[1]}=-[2]^2D_0/A$ (the product would give $-[3]D_0/A$), and the tableau sum is needed.
\item The single rows of $E$ and $B$ follow: $E_{\lambda\mu}=N_{\lambda\lambda}\,(N^{-1})_{\lambda\mu}$,
$B=\Et\Lam N$; in particular \eqref{eq:kntz} is the statement that the single block of $\Et\Lam N$ is the KNTZ
matrix, which we checked but did not derive.
\end{itemize}

\begin{observation}[the single sector as $BC$ interpolation: three determinants and a product]\label{obs:bc}
Fix $n\ge\ell(\nu)$ (the values do not depend on $n$) and put $s=Aq^{1-2n}$, $\beta_l=sq^{2l}+(sq^{2l})^{-1}$,
$k_i(\nu)=\nu_i+n-i$ ($i=1,\dots,n$), $p_k(b)=\prod_{l<k}(b-\beta_l)$ and $V(\nu)=\prod_{i<j}(\beta_{k_i(\nu)}-\beta_{k_j(\nu)})$.
The nodes are $sq^{2k_i(\nu)}=Aq^{2(\nu_i-i)+1}$.  Then for single channels
\begin{gather}
N_{\nu\mu}=c_\mu\,\frac{\det\bigl[p_{k_j(\mu)}(\beta_{k_i(\nu)})\bigr]_{i,j\le n}}{V(\nu)},\qquad
\Et_{\mu\nu}=\frac{V(\nu)}{c_\mu}\det\bigl[a_{k_i(\mu),k_j(\nu)}\bigr]_{i,j\le n},\label{eq:Ndet}\\
a_{kl}=\prod_{\substack{0\le m\le k\\ m\ne l}}\frac1{\beta_l-\beta_m}\quad(l\le k),\qquad a_{kl}=0\quad(l>k),\notag\\
\Phi_\mu(m)=\frac{\det\Bigl[\sum_{l=0}^{k_i(\mu)}a_{k_i(\mu),l}\,g(l)^m\,\beta_l^{\,n-t}\Bigr]_{i,t\le n}}
{c_\mu\prod_{j=1}^ng(n-j)^m},\qquad g(l)=A^{2(l-n)}q^{2(l-n)(l-n+1)},\label{eq:Phidet}
\end{gather}
with $c_\mu=\varphi_\mu(-1)/P^*_\mu(\mu)$, $P^*_\mu(\nu)$ the determinant ratio in \eqref{eq:Ndet} and $\varphi_\mu(-1)$ the
product \eqref{eq:phiprod}.  So the single block of $N$ is Okounkov's $BC$ interpolation polynomial at the Schur point
($q=t=q^2$ in his notation) evaluated at the label, $\Et$ is its inverse by divided differences, and $\Phi_\mu(m)$ is one
$n\times n$ determinant by Cauchy--Binet, the KNTZ $F$-function for every $\mu$.  This identifies the tableau sum
\eqref{eq:tab}.  The same block is also a factorial N\"agelsbach--Kostka determinant in the windowed elementary functions
of Observation~\ref{obs:interp}: $N_{\lambda\mu}=N_{\mu\mu}P_\mu(\lambda)/P_\mu(\mu)$ with
$P_\mu(\lambda)=\det\bigl[e^{[\mu'_i-i]}_{\mu'_i-i+j}-e^{[\mu'_i-i]}_{\mu'_i-i-j}\bigr]_{i,j\le\mu_1}$ at the single
$\lambda$, where a negative window $[m]$, $m<0$, means that the factors of $\{m,\dots,-1\}$ are divided out.  The colour binomial of a single is then the finite sum $\cf R\lambda=\rho(\lambda)^{-1}\sum_{\nu\subseteq
\lambda}\Et_{\lambda\nu}\chih_\nu(R)$ with closed coefficients, for every $R$, and when $s_{R/\lambda}=s_W$ is a single
Schur function (multiplicity $m_\lambda(R)=\langle s_{R/\lambda},s_{R/\lambda}\rangle=1$) it is a product:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:colprod}
\cf R\lambda=\frac{H_q(R)}{H_q(\lambda)\,H_q(W)}\prod_{b\in R/\lambda}\frac{D_{\delta_\lambda(b)}}{D_{c(b)}},\qquad
\delta_\lambda(i,j)=(\lambda_i-i)-(\lambda'_j-j),\quad H_q(X)=\prod_{b\in X}[h(b)],
\end{equation}
$\delta_\lambda$ being the index of \eqref{eq:phiprod} continued to the cells outside $\lambda$.  For a rectangle $R$ every
$\lambda\subseteq R$ has $m_\lambda(R)=1$, and $Z_\lambda\cf R\lambda$ is the figure-eight term of KNTZ.  For a pair
$c=\{Z,Z'\}$ with one minimal diagram $R_c$ and $s_{R/Z}=s_{R/Z'}=s_W$, $s_{R_c/Z}=s_{R_c/Z'}=s_{W_c}$ (any number of moved
cells), $\cf Rc=\cf RZ\cf R{Z'}/(\cf{R_c}Z\cf{R_c}{Z'})$.

\emph{Checks.}  Script \LMPH{bcdet}: \eqref{eq:Ndet} against \eqref{eq:tab}, $\Et$ against the inverse block, $\Phi_\mu(m)$
against $\sum_\nu\Et_{\mu\nu}\Lam_\nu^m$ ($|m|\le4$) and the laws $\Phi(-1)=1$, $\Phi(0)=0$, $\Phi(1)=M(\mu)$; with
\LMPH{--nmax 9}: $1495$, $1591$, $864$ and $96$ cases, no failure, and the N\"agelsbach--Kostka form on the same $1495$
entries; independence of $n$ at $n=\ell+1$.  Script \LMPH{colprod --nmax 7}
\eqref{eq:colprod} on all $249$ cases with $m_\lambda(R)=1$ against the finite sum, the pair rule on all $39$
cases of clean pairs with one minimal diagram in its class against the engine rows, and every rectangle end to end from
\eqref{eq:colprod} and \eqref{eq:Phidet} alone ($151$ stored values, $16$ Rosso--Jones values), no failure.  Analysis
(modulo $p$, two points): \eqref{eq:Ndet} $2749/2749$ and $\Et$ $2887/2887$ up to ten boxes, \eqref{eq:Phidet} $1242/1242$,

nodes $Aq^{2(R_i-i)+1}$ fails in all $2611$ cases up to ten boxes (shifting the nodes rescues only the one-row or only the
one-column diagrams).
\end{observation}

\section{The pair sector: what holds and what is missing}\label{sec:pairs}

A pair $c=\{Z,Z'\}$ has $V=Z\cap Z'$, $U=Z\cup Z'$; it is a \emph{one-cell} pair if $|U|=|Z|+1$, i.e.\ $Z=V+b$,
$Z'=V+b'$ for two addable boxes $b\ne b'$ of $V$.  Put $s=c(b)+c(b')$.  Then $\Lam_c=\Lam_U A^{-2}q^{-2s}$ and
$\Lam_U-\Lam_c=\Lam_U A^{-1}q^{-s}D_s$.  Up to six boxes there are $17$ one-cell pairs and three two-cell pairs
($\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$, $\{[1,1,1,1],[2,2]\}$, $\{[2,2],[4]\}$).

\begin{observation}[all pair rows]\label{obs:all}
$B_{cc}=\Lam_c$, $B_{cU}=0$, $\sum_X B_{cX}=0$, and the row is supported on the channels $X<c$ (singles
$\subseteq U$ and pairs whose union is a proper subdiagram of $U$).
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[one-cell pairs, $17/17$]\label{obs:one}
The vacuum and the $[1]$ entries coincide with the KNTZ entries of the union diagram,
\[
B_{c\vac}=B_{U\vac}=(-1)^{|U|}A^{2|U|}q^{2\kappa_U},\qquad B_{c,[1]}=B_{U,[1]},
\]
and the remaining entries are a correction proportional to $\Lam_U-\Lam_c$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corr}
B_c=B_U-\Lam_U e_U+\Lam_c e_c+(\Lam_U-\Lam_c)\,v_c,\qquad v_{c\vac}=v_{c,[1]}=v_{cU}=v_{cc}=0,\qquad \sum_X v_{cX}=1 .
\end{equation}
(The sum rule is forced by the row sums.)  For the three smallest pairs the vectors $v_c$ are listed in
Appendix~A.3: their entries are of the form $\pm q^a/\{q^n\}$ or $\pm q^a/(AD_k)$ except at one column
($[2,1]$ for $\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}$ and $\{[2,1],[3]\}$), where a polynomial of degree one in $A^2$ appears.  For larger
pairs the number of such non-product entries grows (between $2$ and $7$ non-product entries per row of $B$ for the
one-cell pairs with $|U|=5,6$; $6$ for each two-cell pair).
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[two-cell pairs]
Only Observation~\ref{obs:all} holds in general.  The union identities $B_{c\vac}=B_{U\vac}$, $B_{c,[1]}=B_{U,[1]}$
hold for $\{[1,1,1,1],[2,2]\}$ and $\{[2,2],[4]\}$ but fail for $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$, whose eigenvalue $A^6$ is resonant
with the single $[2,1]$.  (Up to eight boxes, modulo $p$: they hold for all $56$ one-cell pairs and for $16$ of the $22$
multi-cell pairs; the six failures $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$, $\{[1^4],[3,1]\}$, $\{[2,1,1],[4]\}$, $\{[1^5],[3,1,1]\}$,
$\{[2,1^3],[4,1]\}$, $\{[3,1,1],[5]\}$ are exactly the pairs in which $Z$ and $Z'$ differ by a vertical and a horizontal
domino, i.e.\ the resonant ones.  For them the identities hold with the union replaced by the minimal diagram:
$B_{c\vac}=B_{R_{\min}\vac}$ and $B_{c,[1]}=B_{R_{\min},[1]}$ for every clean pair up to eleven boxes, $221$ non-resonant and
$21$ resonant, two points.)  In the analysis (two independent implementations, not re-checked by the
script), the single columns of every non-resonant two-cell pair row of $N$ are a combination of the closed single
rows of the diagrams between $V$ and $U$, the pair analogue of \eqref{eq:signflip}: with $X=Z\setminus V$,
$Y=Z'\setminus V$ ($|X|=|Y|=n\le2$), $z_e=Aq^{2c(e)}$, $u_e=z_e^{\pm1}$ according to whether $e\in\lambda$ or not,
$\mathrm{BC}(t)=t+t^{-1}$, and $l_e(\lambda)=Aq^{2c(e)\pm1}$ the variable of the line of $\lambda$ through $e$ in the
direction transverse to the dominoes of $c$ (rows: $Aq^{2\lambda_i-2i-1}$; columns: $Aq^{-2\lambda'_j+2j+1}$),
\[
N_{c\mu}+\sum_{U_X=\mu}N_{cX}=\sum_{V\subseteq\lambda\subseteq U}M_\lambda N_{\lambda\mu},\qquad
2M_\lambda=\frac{(-1)^n\prod_{x\in\mathrm{ends}(X)}\prod_{y\in Y}\{\sqrt{u_y/u_x}\}}{\prod_{e\in X\cup Y}\{u_e\}}
\prod_{S\in\{X,Y\}}\prod_{a<b\in S}\frac{\mathrm{BC}(l_a)-\mathrm{BC}(l_b)}{\mathrm{BC}(z_a)-\mathrm{BC}(z_b)},
\]
$\mathrm{ends}(X)$ the two end cells of $X$ (for $n=1$ the single cell twice, and the formula is \eqref{eq:signflip}).
The transverse direction is the only one in which the $2n$ cells lie in distinct lines; for the mixed pairs (a domino
on one side, two separate cells on the other) it is fixed by the domino, and the sum rules $\sum_\lambda M_\lambda=1$,
$\sum_{\lambda\ni e}M_\lambda=\frac12$ (consequences of \eqref{eq:Nvac}) force this choice.  Verified exactly for the
$6$ two-cell pairs with at most seven boxes and modulo $p$ for all $28$ two-cell pairs with at most nine boxes at two
points, with no failure.  Open: pairs with two separate cells on both sides (first at ten boxes, no data), the resonant
pairs (their rows have support outside $U$ and no such expansion), and the middle coefficients for $n\ge3$.
\end{observation}

\subsection*{One-cell pairs: what is closed}

Throughout $c=\{Z,Z'\}$ is a one-cell pair, $Z=V+b$, $Z'=V+b'$, $c(b)<c(b')$, $s=c(b)+c(b')$, $d=c(b')-c(b)\ge2$,
$U=Z\cup Z'$, and $x=A^2$, $y=q^2$.  A pair $Y=\{W,W'\}<c$ has its own $U_Y=W\cup W'$ and $s_Y$; the column $U_Y$ is called
\emph{resonant} for $c$ because $\Lam_Y=\Lam_{U_Y}$ at $x=y^{-s_Y}$ (and then $\Phi_Y=\Phi_{U_Y}$).

\begin{observation}[union formula; all non-resonant columns]\label{obs:union}
For every column $X$ that is neither a pair $Y<c$ nor the union $U_Y$ of such a pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union}
B_{cX}=\frac{B_{UX}}{A^2q^{2s}}+\Lam_c\bigl(\delta_{Xc}-\delta_{XU}\bigr)+\frac{A\,D_s}{\{q^d\}}\bigl(B_{ZX}-B_{Z'X}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with the three KNTZ rows \eqref{eq:kntz} of $U$, $Z$, $Z'$ on the right (their pair entries are zero).  These columns are
the vacuum, $[1]$, $U$, $c$ itself and every single $\mu\subseteq U$ that is not the union of two incomparable
subdiagrams of $U$; the entries at $X=\vac$, $[1]$ reduce to $B_{cX}=B_{UX}$ of Observation~\ref{obs:one}.  In terms of
\eqref{eq:corr}: $v_c=f_c+\sum_{Y<c}r_{cY}\,\dfrac{e_Y-e_{U_Y}}{x-y^{-s_Y}}$ with the $x$-free part
$f_c=e_U-M_U+\bigl(y^{-c(b')}M_Z-y^{c(b')-2c(b)}M_{Z'}\bigr)/(y^d-1)$, $M_\lambda:=B_\lambda/\Lam_\lambda$, and one
residue $r_{cY}\in\mathbb Q(y)$ per pair $Y<c$; the pair column $Y$ and its union column $U_Y$ carry opposite residues, so
$B_{cY}+B_{c,U_Y}$ is pole-free.  Checked exactly on all $28$ one-cell pairs with at most seven boxes ($1856$ entries)
and modulo $p$ on all $41$ full-rank one-cell pairs with at most eight boxes ($5704$ entries) in the analysis; the
script (\LMPH{union}) re-checks the $472$ non-resonant entries of the $17$ pairs with $|R|\le6$.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[the residues]\label{obs:res}
The information in a one-cell pair row that is not given by \eqref{eq:union} is the list of residues $r_{cY}$, one per
pair $Y<c$: $123$ elements of $\mathbb Q(y)$ for the $28$ one-cell pairs with at most seven boxes.  The first one is closed,
$r_{c,\{[1,1],[2]\}}=(-1)^{|V|+1}q^{-2\kappa_V}$ (all $27$ pairs that have the column); the others depend on $c$ and $Y$
jointly (not on $V$ and $Y$ alone, and not as a product $a_cb_Y$), and six of them are not products of quantum numbers
and monomials, the smallest being $r_{cY}=q^2(q^6+q^2+1)$ for $c=\{[2,1,1,1],[2,2,1]\}$, $Y=\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}$ and
its transpose.  The adjacent non-landing residues are given by the $K$-hook law (Observation~\ref{obs:khook});
no rule for the landing ones was found.  (The counts are from the analysis; the script checks the $K$-hook law.)
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[hook family; whole rows closed]\label{obs:hook}
For $c=\{[k-1,1],[k]\}$ ($U=[k,1]$, $\Lam_c=A^{2k}q^{2k(k-2)}$, $\Lam_U-\Lam_c=\Lam_cAq^{k-2}D_{k-2}$), with
$\tilde X_j:=\{[j-1,1],[j]\}$ and $\binom{n}{m}_q=[n]!/([m]![n-m]!)$, the correction vector of \eqref{eq:corr} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hook}
\begin{aligned}
v_c([j])&=\frac{(-1)^{k-j+1}\,q^{-(k-j)(k-1)-j}}{\{q^j\}}\binom{k-2}{j-2}_q\quad(2\le j\le k),\qquad
v_c([1,1])=\frac{(-1)^k\,q^{2-(k-1)(k-2)}}{\{q^2\}},\\
v_c(\tilde X_j)&=\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{A\,q^{(k-j)(k-1)+j-2}D_{j-2}}\binom{k-2}{j-2}_q,\qquad
v_c([j,1])=-A^2q^{2j-4}\,v_c(\tilde X_j)-q^{2j+2}\,v_c([j+1])\quad(2\le j\le k-1),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and $v_c=0$ at $\vac$, $[1]$, $U$, $c$.  The transposed family $\{[2,1^{k-2}],[1^k]\}$ follows by $q\to-1/q$ (the
transposition symmetry $B_{c^TX^T}(A,q)=B_{cX}(A,-1/q)$, which holds on every row of $B$ at $|R|\le6$).  So for
$R=[r,1]$ and $[2,1^{r-1}]$, whose channels are singles and these pairs only, the twist-knot formula is completely
closed.  Found on $k\le5$, exact for $k=6$ (not used in finding it), and modulo $p$ for the engine rows of $R=[7,1]$,
$[8,1]$, $[9,1]$, $[10,1]$, i.e.\ $k\le10$, in the analysis; the script (\LMPH{hook}) checks $k\le5$ and the transposes.
Note the two-term structure of the columns $[j,1]$: one term with the resonance pole $D_{j-2}$ of the pair $\tilde X_j$,
one with the quantum pole $\{q^{j+1}\}$; the residues of this family are
$r_{c\tilde X_j}\propto\binom{k-2}{j-2}_q$, a quantum binomial, which is where the ansatz ``pair column
$=$ monomial$/(\Lam_{U_Y}-\Lam_Y)$'' of earlier versions of this note went wrong.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[the binomial matrix on one-cell pairs]\label{obs:Npair}
With $P^c_\mu:=2N_{c\mu}(-A)^{|\mu|}q^{\kappa_\mu}\prod_{b\in\mu}[h_\mu(b)]$ (the normalisation of \eqref{eq:tab}, with the
factor $2$ for the two composites of the pair):
\begin{align}
N_{c\vac}&=1,\qquad N_{c,[1]}=-\frac1{2A}\sum_{b\in Z\sqcup Z'}D_{2c(b)}\qquad\text{(every channel, singles included)},\label{eq:Nvac}\\
N_{cc}&=\varphi_c(-1)=-\frac12\,(-A)^{-|U|}q^{-\kappa_U}\{q^d\}^2\prod_{b\in U\setminus\{b,b'\}}D_{a_U(b)-l_U(b)+2c(b)}
=-\frac{N_{UU}\,\{q^d\}^2}{2\,D_{2c(b)}D_{2c(b')}},\label{eq:Ndiag}\\
N_{c,\{[1,1],[2]\}}&=\frac{[d]^2\{q\}^2D_s^2}{2A^3D_0},\label{eq:Nfirst}\\
P^c_{[2]}&=P_{[2]}(Z)+P_{[2]}(Z')-\{q^d\}^2,\qquad P^c_{[1,1]}=P_{[1,1]}(Z)+P_{[1,1]}(Z')-\{q^d\}^2 .\label{eq:Nadd}
\end{align}
For the larger single columns the same additive structure holds with a correction
$P^c_\mu=P_\mu(Z)+P_\mu(Z')-\{q^d\}^2\Gamma_c(\mu)$, where $\Gamma_c(\mu)$ is a Laurent polynomial in $A,q$ at the
non-resonant columns (e.g.\ $\Gamma([1,1,1])=[2]D_{-3}$ for $\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}$, $\Gamma([3])=[2]D_3$ for
$\{[2,1],[3]\}$, $\Gamma([2,1])=([4]+[2])D_0$ for $\{[2,1,1],[3,1]\}$) and has the pole $D_{s_Y}$ at the resonant ones;
$\Gamma_c(\mu)$ is given in closed form by the four-row formula \eqref{eq:fourrow} below.  Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:Nvac}--\eqref{eq:Nadd}
are checked by the script (\LMPH{binom}, \LMPH{pairn}: $17$ pairs at $|R|\le6$); \eqref{eq:Ndiag} is exact for all $28$
one-cell pairs up to seven boxes, \eqref{eq:Nfirst} for all $20$ pairs at $|R|\le6$ in the analysis (for the two-cell
pairs $N_{c,\{[1,1],[2]\}}=\varphi_{\{[1,1],[2]\}}(-1)\,([3]D_{s/2}/D_0)^2$ with $s$ the content sum of the four moved boxes).
Note that $\Lam_c=\Lam_V\,(Aq^{s})^2$: the pair has the eigenvalue of $V$ with one fictitious box of content $s/2$, and
$D_s=D_{2\cdot s/2}$ is the ``content factor'' of that box in \eqref{eq:union}, \eqref{eq:Ndiag}, \eqref{eq:Nfirst}.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[four-row formula: all single columns of a one-cell pair row]\label{obs:fourrow}
For every single column $\mu\subseteq U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourrow}
2N_{c\mu}=\frac{D_s^2\,(N_{Z\mu}+N_{Z'\mu})-\{q^d\}^2\,(N_{U\mu}+N_{V\mu})}{D_{2c(b)}\,D_{2c(b')}}
=N_{Z\mu}+N_{Z'\mu}+\{q^d\}^2\,\frac{N_{Z\mu}+N_{Z'\mu}-N_{U\mu}-N_{V\mu}}{D_{2c(b)}\,D_{2c(b')}}
\end{equation}
(the two forms agree because $D_{2c(b)}D_{2c(b')}+\{q^d\}^2=D_s^2$), where the four single rows are given by
\eqref{eq:tab} and the left side is read in the merged basis: at a resonant column $\mu=U_X$ it is
$2\bigl(N_{c\mu}+\sum_{X<c:\,U_X=\mu}N_{cX}\bigr)$, the coefficient of $\Phi_\mu$ in the basis
$\{\Phi_\mu,\ \Phi_X-\Phi_{U_X}\}$, and at $\mu=U$ it is $2N_{cc}$.  So the pair row is the average of the two
composite rows $Z$, $Z'$ plus $\{q^d\}^2$ times the divided mixed second difference of the single-row function
$\lambda\mapsto N_{\lambda\mu}$ over the square $V\subset Z,Z'\subset U$, with the steps $D_{2c(b)}$, $D_{2c(b')}$ of
the two added boxes.  Equivalently, with the cell variables $z_e=Aq^{2c(e)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signflip}
2N_{c\mu}=\sum_{u\in\{z_b,z_b^{-1}\}}\ \sum_{v\in\{z_{b'},z_{b'}^{-1}\}}\frac{(u-v)(u^{-1}-v^{-1})}{\{u\}\{v\}}\,
N_{\lambda(u,v),\mu},\qquad \lambda(u,v)=V+[\,b\text{ if }u=z_b\,]+[\,b'\text{ if }v=z_{b'}\,],
\end{equation}
a sum over the four sign choices of the two cell variables (the coefficient is $-\{q^d\}^2/(D_{2c(b)}D_{2c(b')})$ at
$U$ and $V$, $+D_s^2/(D_{2c(b)}D_{2c(b')})$ at $Z$ and $Z'$).  Special cases: $\mu=\vac$ gives $2=2$; $\mu=[1]$ gives
\eqref{eq:Nvac}; $\mu=U$ gives \eqref{eq:Ndiag}; $\mu=[1,1],[2]$ give \eqref{eq:Nadd}; and in general
$\Gamma_c(\mu)=(P_\mu(Z)+P_\mu(Z')-P_\mu(U)-P_\mu(V))/(D_{2c(b)}D_{2c(b')})$ in the merged basis, which closes the
correction of Observation~\ref{obs:Npair}.  Checked by the script (\LMPH{fourrow}: $17$ pairs, $179$ columns at
$|R|\le6$; $28$ pairs, $344$ columns with \LMPH{--nmax 7}; no mismatch, the four rows computed from \eqref{eq:tab});
in the analysis exact on the same rows and modulo $p$ for $56$ pairs ($869$ columns) at eight boxes at two points and
$83$ pairs ($1550$ columns) at nine boxes.  Found on the rows with at most six boxes; the rest was not used in finding
it.  Together with Observation~\ref{obs:khook} below this makes every entry of a one-cell pair row of $N$ closed except
the landing pair entries and the non-adjacent pair entries that depend on them.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[$K$-hook law for the pair columns of $N$]\label{obs:khook}
Write $h_c:=(\text{row of }b',\ \text{column of }b)\in V$ for the \emph{hinge} of $c$ (so that $a_U-l_U+2c$ of $h_c$
equals $s$), and for a one-cell pair $X=\{W,W'\}<c$ with $U_X\subseteq U$ write $V_X$, $b_X$, $b'_X$, $h_X$, $s_X$ for
the same data of $X$ and $K:=\{b,b'\}\cap\{b_X,b'_X\}$ for the moved cells the two pairs share.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:khook}
N_{cX}=-\frac12\,(-A)^{-|U_X|}q^{-\kappa_{U_X}}\,\frac{\{q^d\}^2D_s^2}{D_{s_X}}\;
\prod_{x\in V_X\setminus\{h_X\}}\frac{[h_{V+K}(x)]}{[h_{V_X+K}(x)]}\,D_{a_U(x)-l_U(x)+2c(x)}
\end{equation}
holds (a) on the diagonal $X=c$, where it is \eqref{eq:Ndiag}; (b) in the first pair column $X=\{[1,1],[2]\}$, where
the product is empty and it is \eqref{eq:Nfirst}; (c) for every \emph{adjacent non-landing} entry, i.e.\
$|U|=|U_X|+1$ and $h_c\notin V_X\setminus\{h_X\}$.  It is the pair analogue of the hook product form of
\eqref{eq:tab}: $(\lambda,\mu)\to(V+K,V_X+K)$ in the quantum numbers, $U$ in the $D$-indices, and the hinge of $X$
replaced by the pole factor $D_s^2/D_{s_X}$.  Between adjacent pairs no channel lies strictly between $X$ and $c$, so
$B_{cX}=N_{cX}(\Lam_c-\Lam_X)/\varphi_c(-1)$ and $E_{cX}=-N_{cX}/\varphi_X(-1)$ are closed too; for $V=V_X+e$ with the
same moved cells ($K=\{b,b'\}$) the residue of Observation~\ref{obs:union} is
$r_{cX}=-q^{-c(e)-2s_X}\prod_{x\in V_X\setminus h_X}[h_U(x)]/[h_{U_X}(x)]$.  Also $N_{cX}=0$ whenever $U_X=U$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:khook} fails at every landing entry ($h_c\in V_X\setminus\{h_X\}$), e.g.\ the product must be
replaced by $[4]D_{-1}/[2]=D_1+D_{-3}$ for $c=\{[2,1,1],[2,2]\}$, $X=\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}$; the landing entries are
covered by the general adjacent formula of Observation~\ref{obs:adjres}, of which \eqref{eq:khook} is the non-landing
case.  It also fails at part of the non-adjacent entries (from the rows whose $V$ contains a $2\times2$ block on).  Checked by the script (\LMPH{khook}; $17$ pairs at $|R|\le6$:
diagonal $17/17$, first column $16/16$, adjacent non-landing $15/15$, the implied $B$ entries $15/15$, the zero rule
$6/6$; with \LMPH{--nmax 7}, $28$ pairs: $28/28$, $27/27$, $27/27$, $27/27$); in the analysis exact for all $28$ one-cell pairs with at most seven boxes (diagonal $28/28$, adjacent
non-landing $29/29$), modulo $p$ for $56$ pairs at eight boxes ($56/56$, $74/74$) and $83$ pairs at nine boxes
($83/83$, $118/118$), with the non-adjacent non-landing entries right in $515$ of $741$ cases and the landing ones in
none.  Found on the exact rows with at most seven boxes; the eight- and nine-box rows were not used in finding it.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[all adjacent pair columns, landing included]\label{obs:adjres}
Let $X<c$ be a one-cell pair with $U=U_X+e$ (adjacent), $k,k'$ the moved cells of $X$, $K=\{b,b'\}\cap\{k,k'\}$,
$H(\lambda)=\prod_{x\in\lambda}[h_\lambda(x)]$, and write $B_{cX}=\Lam_c\,r_{cX}\,q^{s+s_X}D_s/D_{s_X}$ as in
Observation~\ref{obs:union}.  Then, with $[-n]=-[n]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjres}
r_{cX}=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{unless }\{b,b'\}\subseteq\{k,k',e\},\\[2pt]
-q^{-2s_X-c(e)}\,\dfrac{H(U)}{H(U_X)} & K=\{k,k'\}\quad(V=V_X+e),\\[8pt]
-q^{-2s_X-c(e)}\,\dfrac{H(V)}{H(V_X)}\,\dfrac{[d_X+1]\,[d_X-1]}{[d_X]\,[d_c]\,[d_3]^2} & K=\{k\}\quad(V=V_X+k',\ \text{moved cells of $c$: }k,e),
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $d_X=c(k')-c(k)$, $d_c=c(e)-c(k)$, $d_3=c(e)-c(k')$; and $N_{cX}=N_{cc}B_{cX}/(\Lam_c-\Lam_X)$,
$E_{cX}=-N_{cX}/\varphi_X(-1)$.  The $K$-hook law is the case $d_3=\pm1$ ($e$ next to $k'$); the formula covers the
landing entries as well, and its zero rule explains the zero entries seen at eight and nine boxes (a moved cell of
$c$ inside $V_X$).  Checked by the script (\LMPH{adjres}: $27$ adjacent entries at $|R|\le6$, ten of them landing,
and $47$ with \LMPH{--nmax 7}, $18$ landing, on $N$ and on $B$, no mismatch); in the analysis exact for all adjacent
entries up to seven boxes including the six rank-deficient seven-box pairs recomputed at node bound $10$ ($47+30$
entries) and modulo $p$ for $156$ entries at eight boxes and $258$ at nine boxes, at two points, no failure.  Found by
a linear fit of a cell-type product ansatz to $18$ exact residues; three monomial exponents that the small diagrams
leave free were fixed by the eight- and nine-box rows, after which every other entry of those rows is a check.
Together with Observations \ref{obs:fourrow} and \ref{obs:khook} every entry of a one-cell pair row of $N$ is closed
except the non-adjacent pair columns.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[gap two, and the chain relation]\label{obs:gap2}
Write the pair-pair block of $B$ as $B_{cX}=\Lam_c\,T_{cX}\,q^{s-s_X}D_s/D_{s_X}$ with $T_{cX}:=r_{cX}q^{2s_X}\in\mathbb Q(q)$,
$T_{cc}=1$ (so $B$ restricted to the one-cell pairs is $\mathrm{diag}(D_sq^s)\,\Lam\,T\,\mathrm{diag}(D_sq^s)^{-1}$ with a
unitriangular pure-$q$ matrix $T$).  For $|U|=|U_X|+2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap2}
T_{cX}=\sum_{Y}\frac{T_{cY}\,T_{YX}}{1+q^{\,a_1(Y)-a_2(Y)}},\qquad
\frac{\Lam_c}{\Lam_Y}=A^2q^{2a_1(Y)},\quad\frac{\Lam_Y}{\Lam_X}=A^2q^{2a_2(Y)},
\end{equation}
the sum over the one-cell pairs $Y$ with $U_X\subsetneq U_Y\subsetneq U$ and the adjacent $T$'s from \eqref{eq:adjres}
(when the two added cells can be taken in either order the two weights $1/(1+q^{\pm a})$ add up to one).  This
produces the product residues and the non-product ones alike, e.g.\ $T=q^6+q^2+1$ for $c=\{[2,1,1,1],[2,2,1]\}$,
$X=\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}$ from the two intermediate pairs $\{[1^4],[2,1,1]\}$ and $\{[2,1,1],[2,2]\}$.  The entries of $N$
follow from the chain relation of $NB=\Lam N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
N_{cX}\,(\Lam_c-\Lam_X)=\sum_{X<Y\le c}N_{cY}\,B_{YX},
\end{equation}
in which only one-cell pairs $Y$ contribute (single rows have no pair entries, one-cell pair rows no two-cell pair
entries).  Checked by the script (\LMPH{gap2}: $19$ gap-two entries at $|R|\le6$ and $42$ with \LMPH{--nmax 7}, $B$ by
\eqref{eq:gap2} and $N$ by \eqref{eq:chain}, every ingredient from the closed formulas, no mismatch); in the analysis
exact for $66$ entries up to seven boxes and modulo $p$ for $156$ entries at eight and $287$ at nine boxes, two
points, no failure.  Two more facts from the analysis: if both moved cells are shared ($V=V_X+\{e_1,\dots,e_m\}$) then
at any gap $T_{cX}=B^{\rm KNTZ}_{U\,U_X}/\Lam_U$, the KNTZ matrix of the unions; and for gap $m\ge3$ the chain relation
together with the pure-$q$ property of $T$ determines $T_{cX}$ uniquely (the right side of \eqref{eq:chain} must vanish
at every root of $\Lam_c=\Lam_X$), which reproduces every non-adjacent entry up to nine boxes with no failure;
Observation~\ref{obs:roots} turns this recursion into a closed formula at every gap.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[every gap: the root formula]\label{obs:roots}
Let $X=\{W,W'\}<c$ be one-cell pairs with $U_X\subsetneq U$, $g=|U|-|U_X|$ the gap, and
$\Lam_c/\Lam_X=A^{2g}q^{2a}$ with $a=\kappa_Z+\kappa_{Z'}-\kappa_W-\kappa_{W'}$.  Iterating the chain relation
\eqref{eq:chain} gives the path expansion of the triangular inverse,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paths}
N_{cX}=N_{cc}\,q^{s-s_X}\frac{D_s}{D_{s_X}}\sum_{c=Y_0>Y_1>\dots>Y_k=X}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}T_{Y_{i-1}Y_i}\,
\frac{\Lam_{Y_{i-1}}}{\Lam_c-\Lam_{Y_i}}\qquad(k\ge1),
\end{equation}
over all chains of one-cell pairs, with the pure-$q$ factors $T_{YY'}=r_{YY'}q^{2s_{Y'}}$ of the pair-pair block of
$B$ (adjacent steps: the residue formula \eqref{eq:adjres}).  The term $k=1$ contains the unknown $T_{cX}$ times
$\Lam_c/(\Lam_c-\Lam_X)$.  The row $N_c$ has poles only at the resonances $D_{s_Y}$, so the sum must be regular at
every root $A^2=\zeta q^{-2a/g}$, $\zeta^g=1$, of $\Lam_c=\Lam_X$ except where $N_{cc}$ itself vanishes (the root
$\zeta=1$ when $2a/g$ is an even integer, a zero of a $D_k$).  This determines $T_{cX}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roots}
T_{cX}=-\frac1{g-1}\sum_{\zeta^g=1,\ \zeta\ne1}\ \sum_{k\ge2}\ \sum_{c=Y_0>\dots>Y_k=X}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}T_{Y_{i-1}Y_i}
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{\rho_i}{1-\rho_i}\,\bigg|_{A^2=\zeta q^{-2a/g}},\qquad \rho_i=\frac{\Lam_{Y_i}}{\Lam_c},
\end{equation}
one and the same value at every root (this is the pure-$q$ property).  For $g=2$ there is one root, $A^2=-q^{-a}$,
$\rho/(1-\rho)=-1/(1+q^{a_1-a_2})$, and \eqref{eq:roots} is \eqref{eq:gap2}.  Manifestly rational form, used by the
script: with $y=A^{-2}$, $\rho_Y=y^{g_{cY}}q^{-2a_{cY}}$ ($g_{cY}$, $a_{cY}$ the gap and the exponent between $c$ and
$Y$) and $\Psi(y)=q^{m_1\varphi(e)}\Phi_e(y^{g_1}q^{-m_1})$, $e=\gcd(g,2a)$, $g_1=g/e$, $m_1=2a/e$, $\Phi_e$ the
cyclotomic polynomial (the factor of $y^g-q^{2a}$ whose roots are $\zeta q^{2a/g}$ with $\zeta$ a primitive $e$-th
root of unity; it never contains a $D$-zero nor a resonance $\Lam_Y=\Lam_c$), the chain sum is $S=P/Q$ with
$Q=\prod_Y(1-\rho_Y)$, and $P+T_{cX}\,Q\equiv0\pmod\Psi$ fixes $T_{cX}\in\mathbb Q(q)$ and overdetermines it by
$\deg\Psi-1$ further equations (all satisfied on the data).  Then $B_{cX}=\Lam_cT_{cX}q^{s-s_X}D_s/D_{s_X}$ and
$N_{cX}$ by \eqref{eq:chain}.  Checked by the script (\LMPH{roots}; at $|R|\le6$: $27$, $19$, $9$ entries of $B$ and
$N$ at gaps $1$, $2$, $3$; \LMPH{--nmax 7}: $47$, $42$, $25$, $11$ at gaps $1$--$4$; \LMPH{--nmax 7 --nb 10}, i.e.\
with the vanishing conditions imposed on all diagrams with at most ten boxes, where all $34$ one-cell pairs with
$|U|\le7$ have full rank: $77$, $66$, $37$, $17$; no mismatch, every quotient-ring result a constant); in the analysis
exact for the same $34$ rows, and modulo $p$ at two points for the $22$ rows with $|U|=8$ (gaps $1$--$5$: $79$, $90$,
$70$, $38$, $22$ entries), the $41$ rows with $|U|=9$ (gaps $1$--$6$: $138$, $161$, $136$, $95$, $46$, $27$) and the
$55$ rows with $|U|=10$ (gaps $1$--$7$: $251$, $273$, $257$, $189$, $121$, $54$, $31$; $T$, $B$ and $N$ each), plus the
$\Et$ entries whose intermediate rows are rank-deficient in the engine and are supplied by the formula ($287$, $393$,
$401$, $321$, $217$, $102$, $55$ at ten boxes), no failure.  Nothing is fitted; the formula was derived from the chain
relation and the pole structure before the eight-, nine- and ten-box rows were used.  What was \emph{not} found is a
per-chain weight in $\mathbb Q(q)$: the $\zeta$-average of a single chain is rational only when $e=g$, the
$q$-shuffle form $\prod_k\bigl(\sum_{j\ge k}q^{a_k-a_j}\bigr)^{-1}$ over the orderings of the added cells is exact
precisely when all maximal chains carry the same multiset of step exponents (at ten boxes $62$ of $165$ gap-three and
$47$ of $128$ gap-four entries) and never otherwise, and $T_{cX}/M^{\rm KNTZ}_{U\,U_X}$ is not a product in general.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[the pair-pair block as an interpolation matrix; zonal binomials]\label{obs:zonal}
The root average \eqref{eq:roots} has a per-entry form on most columns.  For a one-cell pair $c=\{V+b,V+b'\}$ remove the
two particles at $c(b)$, $c(b')$ from the Maya diagram $\{V_j-j\}$ of $V$ and read the rest as a partition $R(c)$ of
charge $-2$, $\{R_j-j-2\}=\{V_j-j\}\setminus\{c(b),c(b')\}$ (the \emph{row core}); the \emph{column core} is
$K(c)=R(c^T)$.  Then $|R|+|K|=2(|V|-1)$, and $(R,K)$ determines $c$.  With $y=q^2$ put
$F_X(c):=(-1)^{|V_c|+|V_X|}y^{\kappa(V_c)-\kappa(V_X)}\,T_{cX}$, so that $F_X(X)=1$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(I)] For every column $X$, $F_X$ is the unique element of the space $\Pi_d$, $d=|U_X|-3$, spanned by the functions
$c\mapsto f_\nu(R(c))\,m_{ab}(y^{c(b)},y^{c(b')})$ with $|\nu|+a+b\le d$ ($f_\nu(R)=s_{R'/\nu'}(p^\circ)/s_{R'}(p^\circ)$,
$m_{ab}$ the monomial symmetric polynomial in two variables), that is $1$ at $X$ and $0$ at every other pair $Y$ with
$|U_Y|\le|U_X|$.  So $T$ is an interpolation matrix on the lattice of pairs, the pair analogue of the interpolation
property of \eqref{eq:tab}; the zeros $T_{cX}=0$ for $U_X\not\subseteq U_c$ come out by themselves.
\item[(II)] If both cores of $X$ are of axis type, $(R(X)/2,K(X)/2)=(\mu,\nu)$ with $(\mu,\nu)$ one of (at most two columns,
$\varnothing$), ($\varnothing$, at most two columns), (one column, one column), the interpolant is one product:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zonal}
T_{cX}=(-1)^{|V_c|+|V_X|}\,q^{2\kappa(V_X)-2\kappa(V_c)}\;
\cbin{R(c)/2}{\mu}_{y^2,\,y}\;\cbin{K(c)/2}{\nu}_{y^{-2},\,y^{-1}},
\end{equation}
where $\cbin\lambda\mu_{q_M,t_M}=P^*_\mu(\lambda)/P^*_\mu(\mu)$ is Okounkov's binomial coefficient (the interpolation
Macdonald polynomial of Knop--Sahi--Okounkov, here at the zonal parameters $q_M=t_M^2$), evaluated at the half-integral
point $R(c)/2$ and given explicitly by Okounkov's tableau formula.  Every pair with $|U|\le5$ is of axis type.  Examples, at
every gap: $T_{c,\{[1,1],[2]\}}=(-1)^{|V_c|+1}q^{-2\kappa(V_c)}$ and
$T_{c,\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}}=(-1)^{|V_c|}q^{-2-2\kappa(V_c)}\sum_{v\ge0}q^{-2v}\,(q^{2R_v(c)}-1)/(q^4-1)$.
\end{itemize}
The block also has the symmetries $T_{c^TX^T}(q)=T_{cX}(1/q)$ and
$(T^{-1})_{cX}=(-1)^gq^{2\kappa(U_X)-2\kappa(U_c)}T_{cX}(1/q)$.  For the columns that are not of axis type the product form
provably fails: $F_X(c)$ is not of the form $f(R_c)g(K_c)$ ($2767$ non-zero $2\times2$ minors in $89$ columns, every one
involving a pair with $|U_c|\ge12$, so no data below twelve boxes can see it); (I) remains.  For $N$ itself these columns
are explicit in a determinantal sense: $N_{cY}=N_{YY}L_Y(c)$ for every one-cell row $c$ and one-cell column $Y$, axis type
or not, with the Lagrange polynomial $L_Y$ of \eqref{eq:lagrange} (Observation~\ref{obs:twocell}(L); analysis: all $7543$
non-axis entries per point at eleven boxes, two points).  Checked in the analysis, not
by the script: (I) exactly on all $197$ pair entries up to seven boxes, modulo $p$ at two points on every engine entry up
to eleven boxes ($3983$ at eleven boxes), and on the $98\,343$ entries with $|U_c|\le14$ generated by the root principle,
plus $365\,259$ predicted zeros; (II) on the $26\,826$ axis-type entries among them ($2245$ of the $3983$ engine entries
at eleven boxes); no failure.  Nothing is fitted in (I): its conditions use only $T_{XX}=1$ and $T_{YX}=0$.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[the whole row of a one-cell pair is closed]\label{obs:fullrow}
With \eqref{eq:roots} every pair column of a one-cell pair row of $N$ is closed, hence so is the split of the regular

N_{c\mu}=\frac{D_s^2\,(N_{Z\mu}+N_{Z'\mu})-\{q^d\}^2\,(N_{U\mu}+N_{V\mu})}{2\,D_{2c(b)}D_{2c(b')}}

\end{equation}
and then every single column of $B$ from $NB=\Lam N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bsingle}

-\sum_{Y<c}N_{cY}\,B_{Y\mu}\Big),
\end{equation}
the rows $B_Y$ of the smaller pairs by the same formula ($N_{cU}=B_{cU}=0$ come out automatically; at the non-resonant
columns \eqref{eq:Bsingle} agrees with the union formula \eqref{eq:union}, and it covers the resonant columns too).
Together with \eqref{eq:tab} and \eqref{eq:kntz} every entry of $N$, $\Et$, $E$ and $B$ is now closed for every
channel that is a single or a one-cell pair, at any gap, and the twist-knot formula \eqref{eq:channel} is closed for
every $R$ all of whose channels are of these two kinds: all $|R|\le5$; at six boxes all but $[4,2]$, $[3,2,1]$,
$[2,2,1,1]$; at seven all but $[5,2]$, $[4,2,1]$, $[3,2,1,1]$, $[2,2,1,1,1]$; at eight all but $[6,2]$, $[5,3]$,
$[5,2,1]$, $[4,3,1]$, $[4,2,2]$, $[4,2,1,1]$, $[3,3,1,1]$, $[3,2,2,1]$, $[3,2,1,1,1]$, $[2,2,2,1,1]$, $[2,2,1^4]$

$458+458$ with \LMPH{--nmax 7 --nb 10}; no mismatch); in the analysis modulo $p$ at two points for every one-cell pair
at eight ($925$ columns), nine ($1633$) and ten boxes ($2629$), $N$ and $B$ each, no failure.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[one sign-flip kernel for every pair row]\label{obs:kernel}
Let $c=\{Z,Z'\}$ be any pair, $V=Z\cap Z'$, $X=Z\setminus V$, $Y=Z'\setminus V$ the moved cells ($n=|X|=|Y|$), $z_e=Aq^{2c(e)}$,
and for a sign pattern $\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^{X\cup Y}$ put $u_e=z_e^{\sigma_e}$ and $\lambda(\sigma)=V+\{e:\sigma_e=+1\}$.
Then, in the merged basis ($R_c(\mu)=N_{c\mu}+\sum_{W:\,U_W=\mu}N_{cW}$), for every single column $\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
2R_c(\mu)=\sum_{\sigma:\ \lambda(\sigma)\text{ a diagram}}K_c(\sigma)\,N_{\lambda(\sigma)\mu},\qquad
K_c(\sigma)=(-1)^n\,\frac{\prod_{(x,y)\in P(\sigma)}\{\sqrt{u_y/u_x}\}}{\prod_{e\in X\cup Y}\{u_e\}}
\prod_{S\in\{X,Y\}}\ \prod_{a<b\in S}\frac{\{\xi_c\sqrt{u_au_b}\}}{\{\sqrt{u_au_b}\}},
\end{equation}
with the closed single rows \eqref{eq:tab} on the right.  Here $\xi_c=\sqrt{z_{\rm in}/z_{\rm out}}$ over a domino of
$c$ ($q^{-1}$ for a horizontal, $q$ for a vertical domino, the same factor on both sides; $1$ if $c$ has no domino),
and $P(\sigma)=\mathrm{ends}(X)\times Y$ when $n\le2$ or when no or all cells of $X$ carry $+$, $P(\sigma)=X\times
\mathrm{ends}(Y)$ when no or all cells of $Y$ carry $+$, $\mathrm{ends}(S)$ the two extreme cells of $S$ by content
(for $n=1$ the single cell twice).  All square roots are monomials, $\sqrt{u_y/u_x}=A^{(\sigma_y-\sigma_x)/2}
q^{\sigma_yc(y)-\sigma_xc(x)}$.  For $n=1$ this is the sign-flip form \eqref{eq:signflip} of the four-row formula; for
$n=2$ it is the grid expansion of the two-cell observation above with the ``transverse direction'' replaced by the
intrinsic domino shift $\xi_c$, so that no row/column input remains, and the sum rules $\sum_\sigma K=2$,
$\sum_{\sigma_e=+}K=1$, $\sum_\sigma K/\prod_eu_e=2$ (the columns $\vac$, $[1]$ and $\Phi(-1)=\one$) hold identically.
Checked by the script (\LMPH{kernel}: at $|R|\le6$ the $17$ one-cell pairs, $179$ columns, and the two non-resonant
two-cell pairs, $24$ columns; \LMPH{--nmax 7 --nb 10}: $34$ one-cell pairs, $458$ columns, and the six non-resonant

$K_c(k,k')=(-1)^n[n]!^2\{q\}^{2n}\,\mathrm{Num}(k,k')\big/\bigl(\prod_{j\ne k}(X_k-X_j)\prod_{j\ne k'}(Y_{k'}-Y_j)\bigr)$;

\binom nj_q\mathrm{Num}(1,j)=(U-B_{n-1-j})(V-B_{j-1})\sum_{l=0}^{2}\binom2l_q\binom{n-2}{j-l}_q\,
\Pi_{n+3-j,\,j-l}(U)\,\Pi_{j+3-2l,\,n-2-j+l}(V)
\end{equation}
for $0\le j\le n$, and the symmetries $\mathrm{Num}_{k,k'}=\mathrm{Num}_{k',k}=\mathrm{Num}_{n-k',n-k}$,

works on the larger interval $[V,R_{\min}]$.  Merge every resonant pair column into its $R_{\min}$ instead of its union;
then $2R_c(\mu)=\sum_{V\subseteq\lambda\subseteq R_{\min}}K_c(\lambda)N_{\lambda\mu}$, where $K_c$ is a product of the type
\eqref{eq:kernel} on the states $\lambda$ for which $\lambda+(1,1)$ is not a diagram (each domino with its own shift, $q$

($11\,883$ single columns; exact on the three pairs up to seven boxes, modulo $p$ at two points and at $4300$ points of

\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[the rest of a non-resonant two-cell row]\label{obs:twocell}
Let $c=\{V+x_1+x_2,\ V+y_1+y_2\}$ be a non-resonant two-cell pair (row/row, column/column or domino/separate),
$\mathrm{wt}\,c=|Z|$, and sort the columns $Y<c$ by weight ($|Z_Y|$ for a pair, $|\mu|$ for a single).
\begin{itemize}
\item[(D)] Diagonal, for every pair with $n\le2$ moved cells per side and for the line pairs of any $n$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pdiag}
N_{cc}=\frac{(-1)^n}{2}\,(-A)^{-|U|}q^{-\kappa_U}\prod_{x\in\mathrm{ends}(X),\ y\in Y}\{q^{c(y)-c(x)}\}
\prod_{v\in V}D_{a_U(v)-l_U(v)+2c(v)+\delta(v)},
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{ends}(X)$ as in \eqref{eq:kernel}, $\delta=0$ except at the hinge of the separate side of a domino/separate pair
(the row of its upper-right and the column of its lower-left cell), where $\delta=-1$ for a horizontal and $+1$ for a
vertical domino.  For $n=1$ this is \eqref{eq:Ndiag}; e.g.\ $N_{cc}=\frac12A^{-6}q^{-5}\{q^2\}\{q^3\}^2\{q^4\}D_2D_3$ for
$\{[2,2],[4]\}$.  First pair column, for the same pairs: $N_{c,\{[1,1],[2]\}}=\bar E_1(c)^2/(2A^3D_0)$ with
$\bar E_1=\sum_{e\in Z}(z_e+z_e^{-1})-\sum_{e\in Z'}(z_e+z_e^{-1})$.
\item[(Z)] Zeros: $N_{cY}=0$ for a single containing at least three of the four moved cells (so for every single of
larger weight), a one-cell pair whose $V_Y$ contains at least two moved cells of $c$, a resonant pair, a two-cell pair
not contained in $c$ side by side, and every pair with three or more moved cells per side.
\item[(P)] The non-zero pair columns of weight $\mathrm{wt}\,c$ are the \emph{partners} $X=\{Z,Z'-r+m\}$ ($r\in Y$,
$m\in X$, $\sigma=+1$) and $X=\{Z-r+m,Z'\}$ ($r\in X$, $m\in Y$, $\sigma=-1$) whose sides are diagrams, and for a
domino/separate pair one more column $X^*=\{V+d_1+s_1,V+d_1+s_2\}$ ($d_1$ the domino cell next to $V$, $d_2$ the other,
$s_1,s_2$ the separate cells).  No channel lies between them and $c$, so $N_{cX}=N_{cc}B_{cX}/(\Lam_c-\Lam_X)$, and
$B_{cX}/\Lam_c$ is a product.  With the $D$-side the side of $c$ that is a domino on the boundary ($\epsilon=+1$ in row $0$,
$-1$ in column $0$), the $F$-side the other one, and $s'$, $d'$ the cells of the $F$- and $D$-side that stay,
\[
\frac{B_{cX}}{\Lam_c}=\sigma\,\frac{Aq^{c(m)}}{\{q\}}\times
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{[c(r)-c(s')+\epsilon]}{[c(r)-c(s')]\,[c(r)-c(d')]}\;\dfrac{D_{2c(d')}\,D_{c(d')+\alpha}}{D_{c(d')+c(r)+\alpha}} & (r\text{ on the $F$-side}),\\[12pt]
\dfrac{[c(m)-c(s')-\epsilon]}{[c(m)-c(s')]\,[c(m)-c(d')]}\;\dfrac{D_{2c(m)-\epsilon t}\,D_{c(m)+\epsilon t}}{D_{c(m)+c(d')-\epsilon}} & (m\text{ on the $F$-side}),
\end{cases}
\]
quantum numbers of content differences taken with $|n|$; $t=1$, $\alpha=0$ when the $F$-side is two separate cells,
and for an $F$-domino with $f_1$ its cell next to $V$: $t=1+{}$the column (horizontal) or row (vertical) of $f_1$, and
$\alpha=-3\epsilon$ if that coordinate is positive, else $0$.  For the extra column,
\[
\frac{B_{cX^*}}{\Lam_c}=\pm\frac{Aq^{c(d_1)}}{\{q\}}\,\frac{[k-1]\,[k+1]\,D_{c(d_2)}}{\prod_{d\in\{d_1,d_2\},\,s\in\{s_1,s_2\}}[c(d)-c(s)]},
\qquad k=|c(s_1)-c(s_2)|,
\]
with $+$ when the domino is on the $Z$ side.
\item[(K)] The singles of weight $\mathrm{wt}\,c$: the merged kernel value \eqref{eq:kernel} minus the light pair columns
$W$ with $U_W=\mu$.
\item[(F)] The light columns (weight $<\mathrm{wt}\,c$, pairs and singles) are the unique solution of the zeros (Z) and the
\emph{filtration}: the row $\Et_c$ annihilates every polynomial $f$ of weight $<\mathrm{wt}\,c$ in the composite power
sums $\bar O_k=\sum_{e\in Z\sqcup Z'}\{z_e^k\}$ and $\bar E_k=\sum_{e\in Z}(z_e^k+z_e^{-k})-\sum_{e\in Z'}(z_e^k+z_e^{-k})$
(weight $k$) that is even in the $\bar E$'s, $\sum_Y\Et_{cY}f(Y)=0$ (a case of the uniform filtration of
Observation~\ref{obs:resrows}).  This is one linear system per row at each point
$(A,q)$, with closed coefficients and no kernel needed; it is sharp (at weight $\mathrm{wt}\,c$ it fails), and it also fixes
the light part of every clean one-cell and three-cell row, but of only one of the $21$ resonant rows at eleven boxes.
\item[(L)] Entry by entry, the light columns are Lagrange interpolants.  Split the non-resonant two-cell pairs into two
families by the orientation of their domino: $h$ (row/row, and domino/separate with a horizontal domino) and $v$
(column/column, and domino/separate with a vertical domino); the separate/separate pairs, which have no data, belong to
both.  Add to each family the vacuum, the singles and the one-cell pairs.  Let $\Pi_w$ be the span of the monomials
$\prod_i\bar O_{a_i}\prod_j\bar E_{b_j}$ with an even number of $\bar E$ factors and weight $\sum_ia_i+\sum_jb_j\le w$
($\dim\Pi_w=1,2,5,10,21,39,73,128,223,373$ for $w=0,\dots,9$; the dimensions of the parts of exact weight have the
generating function $(P(t)^2+P(t^2))/2$, $P$ the partition function), and let $\Omega^\xi_w$ be the channels of the family $\xi$ of weight
$\le w$.  Then $|\Omega^\xi_w|=\dim\Pi_w$ for every $w\le9$, and the matrix $V^\xi_w=[f(c)]$ of the monomials $f$ at the
nodes $c\in\Omega^\xi_w$ is invertible (at every point tested), so every node $c'$ has a Lagrange polynomial
$L_{c'}\in\Pi_w$, $L_{c'}(c'')=\delta_{c'c''}$ on $\Omega^\xi_w$.  For a row $c$ of the family and a column
$Y\in\Omega^\xi_w$ of weight $w<\mathrm{wt}\,c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagrange}
N_{cY}=\sum_{c'\in\Omega^\xi_w,\ \mathrm{wt}\,c'=w}N_{c'Y}\,L_{c'}(c),\qquad
L_{c'}(c)=\frac{\det V^\xi_w[c'\leftarrow c]}{\det V^\xi_w},
\end{equation}
with $V[c'\leftarrow c]$ the matrix with the row of $c'$ replaced by the values at $c$, and $N_{cY}=0$ for every light $Y$
outside the family.  In words: the column $c\mapsto N_{cY}$ is the element of $\Pi_{\mathrm{wt}\,Y}$ that vanishes on the
nodes of smaller weight and takes the top values $N_{c'Y}$ on those of weight $\mathrm{wt}\,Y$.  The top values are closed:
the diagonals ((D), \eqref{eq:Ndiag}, \eqref{eq:phiprod}), the partners and $X^*$ of (P) at the two-cell nodes, the split
kernel (K) at the single columns, and $0$ otherwise.  So every light entry is a ratio of two determinants of power-sum
values, with no linear system per row and no lower row of $\Et$; given the count, (F) on a family is equivalent to (L),
and the zeros (Z) follow.  The same formula gives the light part of every single and one-cell row (there only the nodes
in $S$ contribute), in particular $N_{cY}=N_{YY}L_Y(c)$ for a one-cell row $c$ and every one-cell column $Y$.  Up to weight
three all nodes are singles or one-cell pairs and the interpolants are explicit, for every non-resonant row:
\[
N_{c,\{[1,1],[2]\}}=\frac{\bar E_1^2}{2A^3D_0},\qquad
N_{c,\{[2,1],[3]\}}=\frac{\bar E_1\bigl(2\beta(q)\beta(Aq^2)\bar E_1-\{q\}\bar E_1\bar O_1-\beta(q)\bar E_2\bigr)}
{4A^4q^2\{q^2\}D_1},
\]
and $N_{c,\{[1,1,1],[2,1]\}}=-q^2\bar E_1\bigl(2\beta(q)\beta(Aq^{-2})\bar E_1+\{q\}\bar E_1\bar O_1-\beta(q)\bar E_2\bigr)
/(4A^4\{q^2\}D_{-1})$, with $\beta(u)=u+u^{-1}$.  From weight $10$ on the monomials are not independent on the
channels: at $w=10$ a family has $616$ nodes for $\dim\Pi_{10}=617$, and the missing direction is the relation
$f_0=\bar E_1\,s_{333}(X|Y)=s_{433}+s_{3331}$, which vanishes on every channel with at most two moved cells per side
(on the three-cell channels it is $\bar E_1\prod_{x,y}(x-y)$, Observation~\ref{obs:interp}).
Here $X$, $Y$ are the values $z_e+z_e^{-1}$ at the moved cells of the two sides, with super power sums
$p_j(X|Y)=\sum_{i<j/2}\binom ji\bar E_{j-2i}$, and $s_{333}$ vanishes in $(2|2)$ variables.  So (L) holds verbatim with
$\Pi_w$ restricted to the channels: the graded dimension of the restriction, $\sum_jp(j)\,\iota(w-j)$ with
$\iota(d)=\frac12(|H_d|+(-1)^d\,|H_d^{\rm sc}|)$, $H_d$ the partitions of $d$ with $\lambda_3\le2$ and $H_d^{\rm sc}$ the
self-conjugate ones among them, equals the number of new nodes of a family for every $w\le14$ ($243$, $373$, $579$,
$865$, $1296$ at $w=10,\dots,14$, against $244$, $376$, $588$, $885$, $1340$ for $\Pi_w$ itself), and no node is
missing.  The Lagrange basis is
triangular, $L_{c'}(c)=0$ unless $c$ lies above $c'$, for nodes of every kind.  The top values at the separate/separate
nodes and at the domino/separate nodes whose domino is not on the boundary (first $\{[3,3],[4,1,1]\}$) are not closed.
In the data every such domino/separate node is a top of a ghost diagram ($[4,3,1]$, $[5,3,1]$, $[5,4,1]$, $[6,3,1]$ and
transposes), its row is free along the ghost, and the diagonal (D), the four partner laws (P) and the law for $X^*$ demand
six different values of that freedom (all $12$ such tops, two points), so these top values do not come from the
vanishing conditions; they enter only the rows above these nodes, and there are none in the data.  The resonant and
three-cell rows need a larger space (Observation~\ref{obs:resrows}).
\end{itemize}
The light columns are not products in general, e.g.\ for $c=\{[2,2],[4]\}$, $X=\{[2,1],[3]\}$ (with $x=A^2$, $y=q^2$)
$B_{cX}/\Lam_c=x(y+1)(xy^2-1)(y^3+y^2+1)/(y^2(xy-1))$; (L) gives them as determinants.  Some columns of the Lagrange
basis are closed (the hook pairs, the one-row and one-column singles, the two-column axis columns, weight four;
Observation~\ref{obs:interp}); for the others no closed formula is known.  $B$ follows from $NB=\Lam N$.  (L) is checked by the script (\LMPH{lagrange}: the node
count to weight $10$; at $|R|\le6$ every light entry of the $48$ clean single, one-cell and two-cell rows, $1770$ entries,
$776$ non-zero; with \LMPH{--nmax 8 --nb 12} $136$ rows, $13\,656$ entries, $4232$ non-zero; the top values read from the
engine rows; the weight-three columns; no mismatch) and in the analysis on $188\,555$ light entries ($40\,258$ non-zero)
of the clean rows up to eleven boxes (two points, and the exact rows up to seven boxes at three points), on $40\,758$
entries of rows rebuilt from the closed top values without reading any row, and at twelve more points, without a
failure.  Beyond weight nine (analysis, two points): $f_0$ vanishes on all $4128$ channel values tested, the node matrices
have full rank ($616$, $989$, $1568$ at $w=10,11,12$), the triangularity holds on $524\,524$ values, and (L) on the
restricted space reproduces $1108$, $750$, $468$ light entries at $w=10,11,12$ of the rows whose minimal diagrams have
two rows (new rows up to fifteen boxes) and $232$ of the eleven-box single rows.  (L) was found on the ten-box rows at one point; the second point, the eleven-box columns of weight six to nine
and the further points were not looked at before the check.  Checked by the script (\LMPH{twocell}: at $|R|\le6$ (D) on $19$ pairs, the first column on $18$, and the two
two-cell rows, $4$ partners and $7$ zeros; with \LMPH{--nmax 7 --nb 10} $40$, $39$, six rows, $16$ partners, $2$ columns
$X^*$, $43$ zeros; with \LMPH{--nmax 8 --nb 12} $72$, $71$, $16$ rows, $44$ partners, $6$ columns $X^*$, $285$ zeros; no
mismatch); in the analysis exact on the six rows up to seven boxes and modulo $p$ at two points on all $44$ (ten boxes)
and $62$ (eleven boxes, $18$ of them new) clean non-resonant two-cell rows: (D) $62/62$, partners $184$, $X^*$ $30$, zeros
$618$, and the full rows from (D), (P), (K), (F), (Z), no failure.  The shifts $t$, $\alpha$ for an $F$-domino rest on the
fewest cases.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[closed light columns: windowed elementary functions of the composite particles]\label{obs:interp}
Several columns of (L) have a closed form.  For a channel $c=\{Z,Z'\}$ (a single has $Z=Z'$) put $x_m=Aq^{2m-1}$,
$\beta_m=\mathrm{BC}(x_m)$, and let
\[
\hat E_c(t)=\prod_{i\ge0}\frac{(1+t\,x_{Z_i-i})(1+t/x_{Z'_i-i})}{(1+t\,x_{-i})(1+t/x_{-i})},\qquad
\hat H_c(t)=\frac1{\hat E_c(-t)}
\]
be the generating functions of the particles $x_{Z_i-i}$ of $Z$ and the inverse particles of $Z'$, normalised by the
vacuum (a cell of content $m$ moves a particle from $m$ to $m+1$).  For a finite set $W$ of integers (a \emph{window})
put $E^W_c(t)=\hat E_c(t)\prod_{j\in W}(1+t\beta_{-j}+t^2)$ and $H^W_c(t)=\hat H_c(t)\prod_{j\in W}(1-t\beta_{j+1}+t^2)$,
$e^W_r=[t^r]E^W_c$, $h^W_r=[t^r]H^W_c$, $[n]=\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, and $(\mathrm{Alt}\,f)(Z,Z')=f(Z,Z')-f(Z',Z)$,
$(\mathrm{Sym}\,f)(Z,Z')=f(Z,Z')+f(Z',Z)$; $\mathrm{Alt}\,e^W_1$ does not depend on $W$ and is written $\alpha_1$.  For
$\ell(Z),\ell(Z')\le n$, $E^{[n]}_c$ is the elementary generating function of $2n$ numbers $y$ with product $1$, and
$e_r(1/y)=e_{2n-r}(y)$ gives $\mathrm{Alt}\,e^{[n]}_n(c)=0$; likewise $\mathrm{Alt}\,h^{[n]}_n(c)=0$ for $Z_0,Z'_0\le n$.
This is the vanishing of the factorial Schur functions of Okounkov--Olshanski in composite form, and it gives the zero
patterns below.  Each formula reads $N_{cY}=N_{YY}F_Y(c)/F_Y(Y)$, with the closed diagonal $N_{YY}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(H)] Hook pairs $X_{k,m}=\{(m+1,1^{k+1}),(m+2,1^k)\}$, $k,m\ge0$:
$F=\mathrm{Alt}\,e^{[k+1]}_{k+1}\cdot\mathrm{Alt}\,h^{[m+1]}_{m+1}$, on every row of both families of (L) (vacuum,
singles, one-cell and non-resonant two-cell pairs).  The explicit columns of weight two and three in (L) are the cases
$(k,m)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)$.
\item[(S)] The singles $1^w$ and $(w)$: $F_{1^w}=\mathrm{Sym}(e^{[w-1]}_w-e^{[w-1]}_{w-2})$, and $F_{(w)}$ the same
with $h$, on both families.
\item[(Z)] One-cell rows against one-cell columns of axis type, $(R(X),K(X))=(2\mu,2\nu)$ (Observation~\ref{obs:zonal}):
$N_{cX}=N_{XX}\,(\bar E_1(c)/\bar E_1(X))^2\cbin{R(c)/2}{\mu}^{BC}\cbin{K(c)/2}{\nu}^{BC}$, with Okounkov's
binomial coefficients of $BC$ type at $(s;q_M,t_M)=(Aq^{-5};q^4,q^2)$ and $(Aq^5;q^{-4},q^{-2})$, the analogue of
\eqref{eq:zonal} for $N$.
\item[(J)] Two-column axis columns, $\mu=2^{k'}1^{k-k'}$, $\nu=\varnothing$ (and the transposes, with $h$): on the singles
and the family of the same orientation, the unique element of $\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{Alt}\,e_i\,\mathrm{Alt}\,e_j\}$
that restricts to $\bar E_1^2\cbin{R/2}{\mu}^{BC}$ on the one-cell pairs.  For $k'=1$ explicitly, with
$\alpha^W_r=\mathrm{Alt}\,e^W_r$ and $(Q,T)=(q^4,q^2)$,
\[
F=\alpha^{[k+1]}_{k+1}\,\alpha^{\{k,k+1\}}_2+c_k\,\alpha^{[k+2]}_{k+2}\,\alpha_1,\qquad
c_k=-\frac{(1-Q)(1-T^{k+1})}{(1-T)(1-QT^k)},
\]
where $c_k$ is the coefficient of $e_{k+1}$ in Macdonald's $P_{(2,1^{k-1})}(Q,T)=e_ke_1+c_ke_{k+1}$.
\item[(G)] The two-cell columns of weight four, $\{[2,2],[4]\}$ ($h$) and $\{[1^4],[2,2]\}$ ($v$), on the singles and
their own family: $F=\bar E_1^4+3\bar E_2^2-4\bar E_1\bar E_3-12\bar E_1^2=12\,s_{22}(\beta_X/\beta_Y)$.
\end{itemize}
The last form uses the moved cells.  With $\beta_e=\mathrm{BC}(z_e)$ at the cells of $X=Z\setminus V$ and $Y=Z'\setminus
V$, the super power sums are $p_k(\beta_X/\beta_Y)=\sum_{j<k/2}\binom kj\bar E_{k-2j}$, so every super Schur function
$s_\lambda(\beta_X/\beta_Y)$ is a polynomial in the $\bar E$, and by Berele--Regev it vanishes in $(n|n)$ variables when
$\lambda\supseteq((n+1)^{n+1})$ and equals $\prod_{x,y}(\beta_x-\beta_y)$ for $\lambda=(n^n)$.  So $G$ vanishes on the
channels with at most one moved cell per side and is $12\prod_{x,y}(\beta_x-\beta_y)$ on the two-cell channels (the
two-cell columns are this rectangle factor times an interpolant of lower weight, closed at weight four only), and the
relation $f_0=\bar E_1s_{333}(\beta_X/\beta_Y)$ of (L) at weight ten vanishes on all channels with at most two moved
cells and is $\bar E_1\prod_{x,y}(\beta_x-\beta_y)$ on the three-cell channels; $f_0$ spans the kernel of the node
matrices at $w=10$.  At $w=11$ four directions are missing in all ($989$ nodes for $\dim\Pi_{11}=993$), and at one point
they vanish on the channels with at most two moved cells of weight $12$ and $13$.  Conjecturally the first element of

\begin{observation}[three-cell pairs, and the line rows at every $n$]\label{obs:threecell}
Notation of Observation~\ref{obs:kernel}, with $n$ the number of moved cells per side and $U=Z\cup Z'$.  For $v\in V$ put
$\mathrm{idx}_U(v)=a_U(v)-l_U(v)+2c(v)$, let $\alpha_S(v)$, $\beta_S(v)$ be the numbers of cells of the side $S$ in the arm
and in the leg of $v$ in $U$, and $\kappa_S=\sum_{e\in S}(a_S(e)-l_S(e))$ with arms and legs taken inside $S$.  When
$m_c(U)=1$ there is exactly one partition $\lambda_c$ with $\langle s_X,s_{\lambda_c}\rangle\langle
s_Y,s_{\lambda_c}\rangle\ne0$ ($s_X$, $s_Y$ the skew Schur functions of the sides), the \emph{effective shape}; next to a
line side it is $(n)$ or $(1^n)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(D3)] The diagonal of every three-cell pair with $m_c(U)=1$, and of every pair of any $n$ with a line side, is
\[
N_{cc}=\frac{(-1)^n}2\,(-A)^{-|U|}q^{-\kappa_U}\,\mathrm{Br}_c\prod_{v\in V}D_{\mathrm{idx}_U(v)+\delta_c(v)},\qquad
\delta_c(v)=\sum_{S\in\{X,Y\}}\frac{(\kappa_S-\kappa(\lambda_c))\,\alpha_S(v)\beta_S(v)}{\sum_{v'\in V}\alpha_S(v')\beta_S(v')},
\]
with $2n$ brackets $\{q^{c(y)-c(x)}\}$, $x\in X$, $y\in Y$: if a side $T$ is a line,
$\mathrm{Br}_c=\prod_{s\in S,\,t\in\mathrm{ends}(T)}$ over the other side $S$; if neither is, all $n^2$ brackets except a
perfect matching of $X$ and $Y$, which is the anti-content matching when both sides are of the same bent type.  For
$n\le2$ this is \eqref{eq:pdiag}.  Ribbon sides have $\kappa_S=\kappa(\lambda_c)$; a side with a separate cell has hinge
shifts.
\item[(LE)] On the rows of a domino plus a separate cell against a line, in the gauge of the zero rule, the single columns
have the kernel form \eqref{eq:kernel} with $P=S\times\mathrm{ends}(T)$ and $\xi=\xi(T)$ on the twelve states where the line
side $T$ is constant (all its cells present or all absent).  The other twelve states are not products of that type, and the
sum rules leave six of their values per row open.  In another gauge (zero at the kernel pivot) the kernel form fails.
\item[(LC)] The heavy pair columns (weight $|Z|$) of a line row are exactly the chain pairs $\{Z_s,Z_t\}$,
$Z_t=V+{}$the first $n-t$ cells of $X$ and the first $t$ of $Y$, all of them non-zero.
\item[(LP)] Its two partners are closed for every $n$.  For a row/row pair with $X$ in row $1$ and $Y$ in row $0$ ($x_1$,
$y_1$ the first and $x_n$, $y_n$ the last cells), move the first cell $m=s_1$ of a side $S$ into the other side and
remove that side's last cell: $X_{x_1}=\{Z,Z'-y_n+x_1\}$ ($\sigma=+1$), $X_{y_1}=\{Z-x_n+y_1,Z'\}$ ($\sigma=-1$).  No
channel lies between, so $N_{cX_m}=N_{cc}B_{cX_m}/(\Lam_c-\Lam_{X_m})$, with
\[
\frac{B_{cX_m}}{\Lam_c}=\sigma\,\frac{Aq^{c(m)}\,[n]\,D_{c(s_1)+c(s_n)+1}\,D_{c(s_1)-2}}{\{q\}\,[c(y_1)-c(x_1)]\,
D_{c(x_1)+c(y_1)-1}};
\]
a column/column row has the ratio $N_{cX}/N_{cc}$ of its transposed pair at $(A,q^{-1})$.  At $n=2$ this is (P).
\end{itemize}
Checked in the analysis modulo $p$ at two points: (D3) on all $46$ three-cell pairs with $m_c(U)=1$ and a unique row up
to twelve boxes ($16$ classes of side types; two seeds and a $24$-point scan in $A$ each, and recomputed from the engine
rows at both seeds), on the $10$ rank-deficient ones in the gauge of
the zero rule, and on seven four-cell pairs with a line side at $13$--$14$ boxes, predicted before they were computed; (LE)
on $240$ states of ten rows, the kernel form on $408$ residual columns; (LC) in $35$ row checks with $n=3,\dots,6$; (LP) on
$154$ entries, $n=2,\dots,6$, the column/column ones and three row/row ones not used for finding.  The bracket rule
without a line side was found on the rows it is checked on.  So the zero rule is also the gauge in which these rows
have the kernel structure.  Open: the anomalous pairs ($m_c(U)=0$, $R_{\min}\ne U$), whose rows have neither a diagonal
rule nor the kernel form; bent sides beyond the diagonal; the other heavy columns (the outer chain pairs of $n=3$ are
products, the middle one is not).
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[the resonant rows, and one filtration for every row]\label{obs:resrows}
For the resonant pairs of Observation~\ref{obs:kernel}(c) ($V=[a+1,1^b]$, $X=\{x_0,x_1\}$ the vertical domino under the
first column, $Y=\{y_0,y_1\}$ the horizontal domino after the first row, $k=(1,1)$, $R_{\min}=U+k$) put
$\lambda_{11}=V+x_0+y_0$, the single with $\Lam_{\lambda_{11}}=\Lam_c$.  Given the lower rows, the whole row of $N$ is fixed
by closed conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(F$'$)] the filtration of Observation~\ref{obs:twocell} one weight higher: $\Et_c$ annihilates the $\bar E$-even
polynomials of weight $\le\mathrm{wt}\,c$, and not those of weight $\mathrm{wt}\,c+1$;
\item[(Z$'$)] zeros: a single is zero iff it contains two of the blocks $X$, $Y$, $\{k\}$; a one-cell pair $W$ iff $V_W$
contains $\{x_0,x_1\}$, $\{y_0,y_1\}$, $\{x_0,k\}$ or $\{y_0,k\}$; a two-cell pair iff $V_W$ contains $x_0$ or $y_0$;
every other column is non-zero;
\item[(H)] the only non-zero columns of larger weight are the three singles $\lambda_{11}+e$ and the three pairs
$W_{ab}=\{\lambda_{11}+a,\lambda_{11}+b\}$, $\{a,b,e\}=\{x_1,y_1,k\}$, and the latter are products with the resonance in
the denominator,
\[
N_{c,W_{ab}}=N_{cc}\,\frac{Aq^{c(e)}\,D_{2c(a)}\,D_{2c(b)}}{\{q^{c(e)-c(a)}\}\,\{q^{c(e)-c(b)}\}\,D_{c(x_1)+c(y_1)}};
\]
\item[(D), (K$'$)] the diagonal and the merged single columns from the extended kernel of Observation~\ref{obs:kernel}(c).
\end{itemize}
For all $21$ resonant pairs with one minimal diagram up to eleven boxes the system has a unique solution, equal to the
engine row at every column (two points, engine rows at eight to eleven boxes; on the three rows up to seven boxes every
condition holds exactly), and $B$ follows from $NB=\Lam N$.  (F$'$) and (Z$'$) alone fix $18$ of the $21$ rows; (H) is
needed in the three rows where a lower resonant column merges into the same single as $W_{ab}$.  The two filtrations are
cases of one rule, observed on every clean row up to eleven boxes ($1019$ rows per point: singles, one-cell pairs, all
kinds of two-cell pairs, three-cell line pairs): \emph{$\Et_c$ annihilates every $\bar E$-even polynomial of weight
$<|R_{\min}(c)|-n_c$, and not all of those of that weight}, with $n_c$ the number of moved cells per side ($n_c=0$ for a
single, where this is the interpolation property).  The zero pattern (Z$'$) was read off at eleven boxes, so the
eleven-box rows are not an independent check of it; (H) was read off the three exact rows and checked on the other $18$.

The light and top columns of a resonant row are not products in general (for instance the top column
$\{[2,1,1],[2,2]\}$ of $\{[1^4],[3,1]\}$ has the irreducible factor $A^2q^6-A^2q^4-A^2+q^8+q^4-q^2$), but they are
Lagrange interpolants on a larger space whose extra coordinates are products.  Write $c(a,b)=\{[a+1,1^{b+2}],[a+3,1^b]\}$.  The family is the set of
channels below $c$, all inside $[a+3,2,1^{b+1}]$; at a level $w<|R_{\min}(c)|-n_c$ the space is $\Pi_w$ plus the columns
$N_{\cdot X}$ of the resonant pairs and of the two-cell pairs of the other orientation below $c$, with either orientation
as the base.  Then every single and one-cell column, and every two-cell column of the base orientation, is the
interpolant (L) on these nodes, and the extra coordinates are closed.  With $x=A^2$, $y=q^2$, $[n]=(1-y^n)/(1-y)$,
$\delta_a=a-a'$, $\delta_b=b-b'$ and $d=\delta_a+\delta_b$,
\begin{gather*}
\frac{N_{c(a,b),c(a',b')}}{N_{c(a,b),c(a,b)}}=(-x)^d\,y^{\binom{\delta_a}2-\delta_a(a-1)+\binom{\delta_b}2}
\binom a{\delta_a}_{\!y}\binom b{\delta_b}_{\!y}\,\alpha_{aa'}\,\beta_{bb'},\\
\alpha_{aa'}=\frac{x-y^{-a-1}}{(x-y^{-a'-1})\prod_{j=a+a'+3}^{2a+2}(x-y^{-j})},\qquad
\beta_{bb'}=\frac{x-y^{b+1}}{(x-y^{b'+1})\prod_{j=b+b'+3}^{2b+2}(x-y^{j})},
\end{gather*}
with $\alpha_{aa}=\beta_{bb}=1$.  With $Y_h[k,j]=\{[k+1,2,1^j],[k+3,1^j]\}$
and $Y_v[i,l]=\{[i+1,1^{l+2}],[i+2,2,1^{l-1}]\}$ the top two-cell columns of $c(k,j)$ and $c(i,l)$, and $N_{c,c}$ for
$(k,j)$ or $(i,l)$ equal to $(a,b)$,
\begin{align*}
N_{c(a,b),Y_h[k,j]}&=N_{c(a,b),c(k,j)}\,\frac{xy^{b-k}[k]\,(x-1)(x-y^{j+1})}{[b+2]\,(x-y^{j-k})(x-y^j)(x-y^{b+1})}
&&(1\le k\le a,\ 0\le j\le b),\\
N_{c(a,b),Y_v[i,l]}&=N_{c(a,b),c(i,l)}\,\frac{xy^{2}[l]\,(x-1)(x-y^{-i-1})}{[a+2]\,(x-y^{l-i})(x-y^{-i})(x-y^{-a-1})}
&&(0\le i\le a,\ 1\le l\le b),
\end{align*}
and every other two-cell column is $0$.  So the light part of a resonant row is closed, given the rows below it.  The
three-cell line rows obey the same rule, with the three-cell columns of the same orientation added to $\Pi_w$; that block
itself is not closed (in the world of two-row diagrams $24$ of its $27$ entries are products).  Checked (analysis, two
points, rows at eight to eleven boxes and the exact rows up to seven): every level uniquely solvable ($610$ per base);
$2178$ one-cell, $322$ two-cell and $2144$ single entries per base; the resonant block $392$ entries; the two-cell
columns $504$ entries; the three-cell line rows $404$ levels, $2258$ entries.  The block formula was found on the rows with
$a+b=4$ and the two-cell columns on $a+b\le4$, before the eleven-box rows with $a+b=5$ were looked at.
\end{observation}

\section{The evolution coefficients}\label{sec:weights}

\begin{observation}[top coefficients are products]\label{obs:top}
For all $29$ diagrams $|R|\le6$ and all channels $X=[Z,Z']\in\Chan(R)$: $w_X(R)$ is a product of a monomial
$A^aq^b$, quantum numbers $[n]$, $\{q\}$ and factors $D_k$ if and only if $Z\cup Z'=R$ (the \emph{top} channels), with
a single exception in $[3,2,1]$, where the resonant pair $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$ (union $[3,1,1]\ne R$) is also a product.
Every other coefficient is such a product times an irreducible polynomial in $A^2$ and $q^{\pm2}$ of degree
$\le|R|-|Z\cup Z'|$ in $A^2$.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[top-weight law: one closed value for all top channels]\label{obs:topweight}
Let $c$ be a channel with a single minimal diagram $R=R_{\min}(c)$: every top channel of $R$ ($Z\cup Z'=R$), and also
the resonant pairs whose minimal diagram is $R$ (e.g.\ $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$, $R_{\min}=[3,2,1]$, the exception in
Observation~\ref{obs:top}).  No channel above $c$ is present at $R$, so in the channel formula
$w_c(R)=F_c(R)/\varphi_c(-1)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:topweight}
w_c(R)=m_c(R)\,d_c\,\rho(R),\qquad
\rho(R)=(-A)^{|R|}\,q^{\kappa_R}\prod_{b\in R}\frac{\{q^{h(b)}\}^2}{D_{c(b)}}
=\frac{(-A)^{|R|}\,q^{\kappa_R}\prod_{b\in R}\{q^{h(b)}\}}{\dim_qR},
\end{equation}
where $d_c$ is the quantum dimension of the composite(s) of $c$, $m_c(R)=\langle s_{R/Z},s_{R/Z'}\rangle
=\sum_Wc^R_{ZW}c^R_{Z'W}$ is the multiplicity of the composite $[Z,Z']$ in $R\otimes\Rbar$ (Koike; it is $1$ for every
channel in the checks of this paragraph), and $\dim_qR=\prod_{b\in R}D_{c(b)}/\{q^{h(b)}\}$ is the quantum dimension of
$R$ ($A=q^N$).  So $w_c(R)/(m_c(R)d_c)$ is the same closed product for all top channels of $R$, singles and pairs alike, e.g.\
$\rho([1])=-A\{q\}^2/D_0$, $\rho([2])=A^2q\{q\}^2\{q^2\}^2/(D_0D_1)$, $\rho([2,1])=-A^3\{q\}^6[3]^2/(D_{-1}D_0D_1)$.  In terms of
the binomial matrix: the interpolation function $\Psi_c(R):=\sum_X\Et_{cX}\psi_X(R)$ of a channel, which vanishes on every
$R\not\supseteq R_{\min}(c)$, takes at its minimal diagram the universal value $\Psi_c(R_{\min})=\rho(R_{\min})$;
equivalently, the colour normalisation of the rows and the twist normalisation $\Phi_c(-1)=1$ coincide.  This holds on
the whole vanishing ideal of $R_{\min}$ (Observation~\ref{obs:monomial}), so a row cannot carry the multiplicity: where
$m_c(R_{\min})=2$ the channel formula gives $w_c=d_c\rho$, and the second copy comes from the block of the diagram
(Section~\ref{sec:cubic}).  Verified exactly for all $49$ such channels with $|R|\le6$ ($29$ singles, $17$ one-cell pairs, the
pairs $\{[2,2],[4]\}$, $\{[1^4],[2,2]\}$ and the resonant $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$); in the script (\LMPH{topweight}) at
$|R|\le6$ and, with \LMPH{--nmax 7 --nb 10}, for $87$ channels at $|R|\le7$; analysis: modulo $p$ at two points for every
channel with one minimal diagram, $|R_{\min}|\le10$, that has a row ($399$: $138$ singles, $182$ one-cell, $26$
domino/domino, $26$ domino/separate, $15$ resonant two-cell pairs, $12$ three-cell pairs), no failure.  The two channels
with two minimal diagrams that have a row at ten boxes ($\{[3,2,1,1],[5,2]\}$, $\{[2,2,1,1,1],[4,2,1]\}$) satisfy it at one
of their minimal diagrams only: there the unsplit row of the vanishing conditions is a sum of two terms.

\emph{Multiplicities.}  $m_c(R_{\min})=1$ for every channel with $|R_{\min}|\le9$, so the factor is invisible in the checks
above.  It is $2$ for every separate/separate two-cell pair (each side two separate cells): then $R_{\min}=Z\cup Z'$,
both $R_{\min}/Z$ and $R_{\min}/Z'$ are two separate corners, and $m_c=\langle h_1^2,h_1^2\rangle=2$.  Such a pair needs
four corners, and the first are the three $2+2$ splittings of the corners of $[4,3,2,1]$: $\{[3,2,2,1],[4,3,1]\}$,
$\{[3,3,1,1],[4,2,2]\}$, $\{[3,3,2],[4,2,1,1]\}$.  Up to fifteen boxes $m_c(R_{\min})\le2$, and the only other channels with
$m_c(R_{\min})=2$ are some three-cell pairs of the same kind, from $[6,4,2,1]$ on (check \LMPH{mult}).  The factor is
confirmed where it first appears: at $R=U=[4,3,2,1]$ the closed block of the other branch (which reproduces Formula~I for
$|m|\le4$ and Rosso--Jones) gives $w_c=2d_c\rho(U)$ on these three tops, through a full $3\times3$ block on them
(Section~\ref{sec:cubic}), and \eqref{eq:topweight} holds on all $14$ tops
and co-tops of $[4,3,2,1]$ and on the $82$ tops and co-tops of eleven other diagrams with eight to ten boxes that contain
a ghost diagram (two points, $192/192$; analysis, Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}).  For a co-top the law holds at each of its
minimal diagrams, with the row split as in Remark~(e) of Section~\ref{sec:universal}.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[vacuum law: the constant term of the twist binomial]\label{obs:vacuum}
For every channel $c$ with a single minimal diagram $R=R_{\min}(c)$ the constant term of $\Phi_c(m)$ in its eigenvalue
expansion \eqref{eq:Phi}, $\Phi_c(m)=\Et_{c\vac}+\sum_{X\ne\vac}\Et_{cX}\Lam_X^{\,m}$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacuum}
\Et_{c\vac}=-\sum_{X\ne\vac}\Et_{cX}=\pi(R),\qquad
\pi(R)=\prod_{b\in R}\frac1{1-A^{-2}q^{-2c(b)}}=\prod_{b\in R}\frac{Aq^{c(b)}}{D_{c(b)}},
\end{equation}
and $\rho(R)=(-1)^{|R|}\,\pi(R)\prod_{b\in R}\{q^{h(b)}\}^2$.  So the minimal diagram fixes both ends of the channel: the
colour normalisation \eqref{eq:topweight} and the constant term of the twist binomial are the same product up to the
squared hook factor.  For the resonant pairs $R_{\min}\ne U$,
and the law holds with $R_{\min}$: a resonant pair behaves like the single $R_{\min}$ in every global quantity checked
($\Et_{c\vac}$, $B_{c\vac}$, $B_{c,[1]}$).  Checked by the script (\LMPH{vacuum}: $49$ channels at $|R|\le6$, $87$ with
\LMPH{--nmax 7 --nb 10}, $142$ with \LMPH{--nmax 8 --nb 12}, and the relation to $\rho$ for every $R$; no mismatch); in
the analysis modulo $p$ at two points on every clean channel of the stored engine rows up to eleven boxes, $380$ channels
($138$ singles, $221$ non-resonant and $21$ resonant pairs), no failure.
\end{observation}

Examples (full tables for $[2,1]$, $[3,1]$, $[2,1,1]$ in Appendix~A.1; all $29$ diagrams in the JSON table):
\begin{align*}
w_{[2,1]}([2,1])&=-\frac{A^3D_{-3}D_{-1}D_1D_3}{D_0}, &
w_{\{[1,1],[2]\}}([2,1])&=-\frac{2A^3[3]^2\{q\}^2D_{-2}D_2}{[2]^2D_0},\\
w_{[3,1]}([3,1])&=\frac{A^4q^2D_{-3}D_{-1}D_0D_2D_5}{D_1}, &
w_{\{[2,1],[3]\}}([3,1])&=\frac{2A^4q^2[2][4]^2\{q\}^2D_{-2}D_0D_4}{[3]^2D_1},\\
w_{[3,2]}([3,2])&=-\frac{A^5q^2D_{-3}D_{-2}^2D_3^2D_5}{D_2}, &
w_{\{[2,2],[3,1]\}}([3,2])&=-\frac{2A^5q^2[3][4]\{q\}^2D_{-3}D_{-2}D_3D_4}{[2]D_2},
\end{align*}
while e.g.\ $w_{[2]}([2])=A^2qD_{-1}D_0D_3/D_1$ and
$w_{[1]}([2])=-A^2[2]D_{-1}\,(A^2q^6-q^6+q^4-1)/(q^2D_0)$.
The factor $2$ in the pair weights is the two conjugate composites $[Z,Z']$, $[Z',Z]$ counted together.  The
polynomial parts are the explicit form of the ``$K^R_{\mu\nu}(x)\in\mathbb Q(q)[x]$, $\deg_x\le|R|-|\mu\cup\nu|$'' of
the project's Formula~I; we did not find a closed expression for them.

\begin{observation}[regularity law]\label{obs:law}
For every diagram $R$ and every channel $X$, with $M_X(R)$ the smallest minimal diagram of $X$ contained in $R$,
\[
w_X(R)=0\ \ (X\notin\Chan(R)),\qquad
K_X(R):=\frac{w_X(R)}{d_X\,\rho(R)}\in\mathbb Q(q)[A^2],\qquad \deg_{A^2}K_X(R)\le|R|-|M_X(R)|,
\]
$\rho$ the product of \eqref{eq:topweight}.  This is \eqref{eq:law} of Section~\ref{sec:onepage}.  It sharpens
Observation~\ref{obs:top} in two ways: the product prefactor is the same $d_X\rho(R)$ for every channel, not only for the
tops, and the bound uses $M_X(R)$, which is $Z\cup Z'$ for a non-resonant channel and larger for a resonant one (for
$\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$ at $[3,2,1]$ it is $[3,2,1]$ itself, so $K_X$ is a constant: this is the exception of
Observation~\ref{obs:top}).  At a top, $K_c(R)=m_c(R)$ is \eqref{eq:topweight}.  The $K_X(R)$ are the polynomials
$K^R_{\mu\nu}(x)$ of Formula~I with the prefactor made explicit.

\emph{Checks.}  (i) On the stored exact weights, all $263$ labels of the $29$ diagrams with $|R|\le6$, at $16$ values of
$A$ (script, \LMPH{regular}): no failure; the bound is attained on all $214$ labels where it is positive; and the factor
$\rho(R)$ is needed: $w_X(R)/d_X$ alone is a polynomial for none of the $263$.  (ii) On the engine's weights at seven boxes,
all $325$ labels of the $15$ diagrams (\LMPH{regular --nmax 7 --nb 10}): no failure.  With the engine's default node bound
the law fails on exactly the $28$ labels of $[4,2,1]$ and $[3,2,1,1]$ that carry the rank-deficient rows of these two
diagrams (Section~\ref{sec:N}).  There the weights still give the right $H_R(\Tw_m)$ for $|m|\le2$, but not for
$|m|\ge3$: at $[4,2,1]$ and $m=\pm3,\pm4$ the default-bound values are not Laurent polynomials in $q$ and violate the
special-polynomial theorem $H_R(A,1)=H_{[1]}(A,1)^{|R|}$, while the values at node bound $10$ are Laurent polynomials, satisfy
it, and equal the other branch's exact eight-box files (analysis; both versions are Laurent in $A$ and agree with the
$\mathfrak{sl}_3$ reduction $H_{[4,2,1]}(q^3,q)=H_{[3,1]}(q^3,q)$, so only the $q\to1$ test and the law tell them apart).
So the law detects a wrong gauge that the low twist knots do not see.  (iii) Above the ghosts the law is used as an equation, and the
weights it produces agree label by label with the verified formula of Section~\ref{sec:ghosts} on every diagram with nine
and ten boxes above the ghosts (Table~\ref{tab:final}); so the verified weights there satisfy it too.
\end{observation}

\section{The universal formula}\label{sec:universal}

We now put the pieces together.  Append the vacuum to the channels ($\Lam_\vac=1$, $d_\vac=1$, $R_\vac=\varnothing$,
$\rho(\varnothing)=1$) and let $N=\Et^{-1}$ be the binomial matrix of Section~\ref{sec:N} on $\{\vac\}\cup\Chan$, lower
triangular for the order $X<c$.  Write $\chih_X(R)=\psi_X(R)+1=\chi_X(R)/d_X$ for the normalised Koike character of the
composite(s) of $X$ at $q^{2(R+\rho)}$ ($\chih_\vac=1$), and define the \emph{colour binomial} and the \emph{twist
binomial} of a channel $c$ with minimal diagram $R_c$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binomdef}
\rho(R_c)\,\cf Rc:=\sum_X\Et_{cX}\,\chih_X(R)=\Psi_c(R),\qquad\qquad \Phi_c(m)=\sum_X\Et_{cX}\,\Lam_X^{\,m}.
\end{equation}

\begin{conjecture}[universal twist-knot formula]\label{conj:U}
For every diagram $R$ and every $m\in\mathbb Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U}
H_R(\Tw_m)=\sum_{c\in\{\vac\}\cup\Chan(R)}Z_c\,\cf Rc\,\Phi_c(m)\;+\sum_{g\in\Gh(R)}Z_g\,\cf Rg\,\Phi_g(m),\qquad
Z_c=d_c\,N_{cc}\,\rho(R_c).
\end{equation}
Here $N$ is the lower triangular matrix for which the two expansions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twobinom}
\chih_X(R)=\sum_cN_{Xc}\,\rho(R_c)\,\cf Rc,\qquad\qquad \Lam_X^{\,m}=\sum_cN_{Xc}\,\Phi_c(m)\qquad(\text{all }X,\ R,\ m)
\end{equation}
hold with (i) $\cf Rc=0$ whenever $c$ is absent from $R\otimes\Rbar$ (the vanishing conditions; at $R=\varnothing$ this
gives $N_{X\vac}=1$, i.e.\ $\Phi_c(0)=\delta_{c\vac}$) and (ii) $\Phi_c(-1)=1$, i.e.\ $\sum_cN_{Xc}=\Lam_X^{-1}$.  Then
(iii) $\cf{R_c}c=1$ (the ideal identity, Observation~\ref{obs:monomial}).  The second sum runs over the ghosts of the
ghost diagrams $U\subseteq R$ with $\mathrm{ex}(U)=1$ (Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}); it is empty unless $R$ contains one of
$[4,3,1]$, $[3,2,2,1]$, $[5,3,1]$, $[3,2,2,1,1]$, \dots\ A diagram $U\subseteq R$ with $\mathrm{ex}(U)\ge2$ or with a top
of multiplicity $m_c(U)=2$ ($[4,3,2,1]$ at ten boxes, $[6,4,1]$ and $[5,4,2]$ at eleven, \dots) contributes instead a
symmetric form on its ideal, $\frac{M(U)}{\rho(U)}K_U(\varphi(m),f(R))$ with $K_U$ its double-braid block at $R=U$
(Section~\ref{sec:cubic}); these forms are open as closed expressions, and their twist-knot content is fixed by the
regularity law (Conjecture~\ref{conj:final}, which contains this conjecture: the ghost terms, the forms of the
diagrams with $\mathrm{ex}(U)\ge2$ and the block of $[4,3,2,1]$ are all part of its single term $t(R)$).
\end{conjecture}

Conditions (i) and (ii) determine the rows of $\Et=N^{-1}$ (each row up to the ghost directions, which the zero rule of
Section~\ref{sec:ghosts} fixes), so \eqref{eq:twobinom} \emph{defines} $N$ and both binomials; the content of the
conjecture is that the constants are $Z_c=d_cN_{cc}\rho(R_c)$, that the ghost terms are the only additions, and the closed
rules for $N$ (Table~\ref{tab:N}).  Remarks:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] Without the ghost terms \eqref{eq:U} is the channel formula \eqref{eq:channel} rewritten, since
\[
Z_c\,\cf Rc=d_cN_{cc}\Psi_c(R)=d_c\sum_XE_{cX}\psi_X(R)=F_c(R),
\]
and the vacuum term is the $1$.  What is new is the reading as one binomial matrix on both sides, the two normalisation
laws at $R_c$, \eqref{eq:topweight} and \eqref{eq:vacuum}, and the closed pieces of $N$.
\item[(b)] Symmetric form.  With the vectors $\chih(R)=(\chih_X(R))_X$ and $\Lam^m=(\Lam_X^m)_X$,
\[
H_R(\Tw_m)=\chih(R)^{T}\,G\,\Lam^{m},\qquad G=N^{-T}\,\mathrm{diag}(d_cN_{cc})\,N^{-1},
\]
a universal symmetric matrix (independent of $R$, $m$ and the knot); the evolution coefficients of \eqref{eq:evol} are
$w_Y(R)=\sum_X\chih_X(R)\,G_{XY}$.
\item[(c)] Special values.  $m=0$: $\Phi_c(0)=\delta_{c\vac}$, $H=1$.  $m=-1$: $H_R(4_1)=\sum_cZ_c\cf Rc$, so $Z_c$ is the
contribution of $c$ to the figure-eight polynomial at $R=R_c$.  $m=1$: $\Phi_c(1)=B_{c\vac}=B_{R_c\vac}
=(-1)^{|R_c|}A^{2|R_c|}q^{2\kappa(R_c)}$ (Observation~\ref{obs:one}, and the two-cell observation for $R_c=R_{\min}\ne U$),
so
\[
H_R(3_1)=\sum_c(-1)^{|R_c|}A^{2|R_c|}q^{2\kappa(R_c)}\,Z_c\,\cf Rc\qquad(\text{no ghosts}),
\]
the trefoil as a sum of monomials times figure-eight terms (checked against the data for all $29$ diagrams with
$|R|\le6$; Rosso--Jones is the check beyond).
\item[(d)] The constants are products wherever $N_{cc}$ is: for singles $Z_\lambda=d_\lambda\prod_{b\in\lambda}
D_{a(b)-l(b)+2c(b)}\{q^{h(b)}\}^2/D_{c(b)}$ by \eqref{eq:phiprod}, e.g.\ $Z_{[k]}=\prod_{i<k}D_{k+i}D_{i-1}$,
$Z_{[2,1]}=D_{-3}D_{-2}D_{-1}D_1D_2D_3$; for pairs by \eqref{eq:pdiag}, e.g.\ $Z_{\{[1,1],[2]\}}=-[3]^2\{q\}^4D_{-2}D_2$.
\item[(e)] Colour binomials.  For one-row $R$, $\cf{[r]}{[k]}=\binom rk_q\prod_{i<k}D_{r+i}/D_{k+i}$ (checked $r\le7$), and
\eqref{eq:U} at $m=-1$ is the cyclotomic formula $H_{[r]}(4_1)=\sum_k\binom rk_q\prod_{i<k}D_{r+i}D_{i-1}$.  The first
binomial is bilinear in the content sums of $R$,
\[
\cf R{[1]}=\frac{\psi_{[1]}(R)}{\{q\}^2}=\frac{D_0\sum_{b\in R}D_{2c(b)}-\{q\}^2\sum_{b,b'\in R}q^{2c(b)-2c(b')}}{D_1D_{-1}}
\]
(checked on all $44$ diagrams up to seven boxes).  In general $\cf Rc$ is given by \eqref{eq:binomdef} from the closed rows of $N$ and the
closed characters; we did not find a tableau formula for it in the variables of $R$.  For a channel with several minimal
diagrams the row of the vanishing conditions splits into one term per minimal diagram inside $R$
(Observation~\ref{obs:topweight}); \eqref{eq:U} then has one term per minimal diagram.
\item[(f)] Rectangular $R$ have only single channels and no ghosts, and \eqref{eq:U} is the cyclotomic expansion of KNTZ.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The ghost layer}\label{sec:ghosts}

For a diagram $U$ let $I_U$ be the space of functions $f=\sum_Xf_X\psi_X$ (the sum over the channels present at $R=U$)
that vanish at every colour $S\not\supseteq U$.  Every channel whose minimal diagram (or one of whose minimal diagrams) is
$U$ gives an element of $I_U$, and
\[
\dim I_U=\#\{\text{tops of }U\}+\#\{\text{co-tops of }U\}+\mathrm{ex}(U),
\]
tops being the channels with $R_{\min}=U$ and co-tops those with several minimal diagrams, one of them $U$.  The
$\mathrm{ex}(U)$ remaining elements, normalised to have zero coefficient on every top and co-top, are the \emph{ghosts} of
$U$: combinations of characters that vanish on every $R\not\supseteq U$ and belong to no channel.  When
$\mathrm{ex}(U)\ge1$ the vanishing conditions leave the rows of some of the tops of $U$ free along the ghosts.  These rows
are fixed by the \emph{zero rule} (coefficient $0$ at $\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$ for a pair top and at $\{[1,1],[2]\}$ for a single
top; for $[4,3,1]$ these are exactly the rows given by the closed formulas, and for the free three-cell rows it is the
gauge in which (D3) and the kernel form hold, Observation~\ref{obs:threecell}), and for $\mathrm{ex}(U)=1$ the ghost
$k=k_U$ enters \eqref{eq:U} as one more term of the same form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ghost}
\cf Rg=\frac{\sum_Xk_X\,\psi_X(R)}{\sum_Xk_X\,\psi_X(U)},\qquad
\Phi_g(m)=\frac{\sum_Xk_X\,(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1)}{\sum_Xk_X\,(\Lam_X^{-1}-1)},\qquad
Z_g=\frac{H_U^{\rm RJ}(3_1)-H_U^{\circ}(\Tw_1)}{\Phi_g(1)},
\end{equation}
with $H^{\rm RJ}_U(3_1)$ the Rosso--Jones value for the trefoil $\Tw_1$ and $H^\circ_U$ the formula without the ghost term
of $U$.  At $R=U$, $m=1$ the ghost term is the only unknown, so the trefoil fixes $Z_g$, and every other value of $R$ and
$m$ is a prediction; as for the channels, $\cf Ug=1=\Phi_g(-1)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Which diagrams.}  Ghosts occur never for diagrams with at most two corners, always for four or more corners,
and for three corners according to the Kerov gaps $(r_1,s_1,r_2,s_2,r_3,s_3)$ ($r_i$ the number of rows of the $i$-th block
of equal rows, $s_i$ the step after it): if $r_2=s_2=1$, iff ($s_1,s_3\ge2$) or ($r_1,r_3\ge2$); if $r_2=1<s_2$, unless
$r_1=1$, $r_3\ge2$, $s_3=1$; if $s_2=1<r_2$, unless $s_3=1$, $s_1\ge2$, $r_1=1$; if $r_2,s_2\ge2$, always.  The first ghost
diagrams are $[4,3,1]$ (eight boxes), $[5,3,1]$ (nine), $[6,3,1]$, $[5,4,1]$, $[5,3,2]$ and the four-corner $[4,3,2,1]$
(ten), and their transposes.  The rule was found on the census of $\mathrm{ex}(U)$ up to $13$ boxes and checked on all $135$
diagrams with $14$ boxes (a first rule found on $\le12$ boxes failed at $13$), and with the exact count of the next item
on all diagrams up to $18$ boxes ($819$ up to transposition).  For three corners the ghost lives, modulo
the ghosts of smaller ghost diagrams, exactly on the channels whose minimal diagram is a proper vertex of the Boolean
cube $[U-\mathrm{corners}(U),U)$ (nine diagrams up to eleven boxes).
\item \textbf{How many ghosts, and their leading part} (analysis).  $\dim I_U$ has an exact form without characters and
without a node bound.  The colours $S\not\supseteq U$ are the union over the corners $(i,j)$ of $U$ of the fat hooks
$\{S:S_i\le j\}$, on which the content power sums are supersymmetric in $i+j$ variables, and the super-Cauchy identity
turns the vanishing into one identity per corner: $f=\sum_Xf_X\psi_X$ lies in $I_U$ iff the weights $\mu_X$ ($f_X$ for a
single, $f_X/2$ for each orientation of a pair, $\mu_\vac=-\sum_Xf_X$) satisfy
\[
\sum_X\mu_X\prod_{k<i}R_X(a_k)\prod_{l<j}R_X(c_l)^{-1}=0\quad\text{for all }a,c,\qquad
R_X(a)=\frac{\prod_{n\in N(X)}(1-na)}{\prod_{w\in P(X)}(1-wa)},
\]
at every corner $(i,j)$, with $P(Z,Z')=\{t^{\alpha_r+1}\}\cup\{ut^{-\alpha'_s}\}$, $N(Z,Z')=\{t^{-\beta_r}\}\cup
\{ut^{1+\beta'_s}\}$ from the Frobenius coordinates $(\alpha|\beta)$, $(\alpha'|\beta')$ of $Z$, $Z'$, $t=q^2$,
$u=A^{-2}$.  By partial fractions the conditions say that $\mu$ kills the residue monomials of the $R_X$ (products of $i'$
particle and $j'$ hole residues, $(i',j')$ a cell of $U$), so $\dim I_U$ is a Hilbert function of the labels in these
coordinates.  The limit $A\to\infty$ keeps the rank (all $355$ diagrams up to $15$ boxes), the further limit $q\to1$
does not (first at $[5,3,2,1]$).  With this the census extends to $18$ boxes, and for three rows,
$U=[s_1+s_2+s_3,s_2+s_3,s_3]$ and the transposes, $\mathrm{ex}(U)$ is closed whenever a gap is at most two or $s_3=3$
($m=\min(s_1,s_2)$):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\max(0,\min(s_1,s_3)-1)$ for $s_2=1$; $\min(s_1,s_2-1)$ for $s_3=1<s_2$; $\min(s_2,s_3)$ for $s_1=1<s_2$;
\item $7(m-1)+3\,[s_2>s_1]$ for $s_3=2\le s_1,s_2$; for $s_2=2\le s_1$, $s_3\ge3$: $9s_1-8$ ($s_1<s_3$), $9s_3-12$
($s_1\ge s_3$); for $s_1=2$, $s_2,s_3\ge3$: $9s_2-6$, $9s_3-9$, $9s_3-8$ for $s_2<s_3$, $s_2=s_3$, $s_2>s_3$;
\item $21m-28$, $21m-32$, $21m-23$, $21m-22$ for $s_3=3\le s_1,s_2$ and $s_1>s_2$, $s_1=s_2$, $s_2=s_1+1$, $s_2\ge s_1+2$.
\end{itemize}
The third difference $\Delta_1\Delta_2\Delta_3\,\mathrm{ex}$ lies in the band $-(s_3-2)\le s_2-s_1\le s_3-1$ with totals
$1,6,14,28$ for $s_3=1,\dots,4$, whose partial sums are the slopes $1,7,21,49$ in $m$ (observed).  The ghost space has a
canonical filtration by depth, $|U|$ minus the largest level $|Z|$ at which a ghost has a non-zero coefficient.  No
ghost has depth $0$ or $1$, the graded dimensions do not depend on the point, and the top piece, depth $2$, has
dimension $1$, $7$, $21$ for three, four, five corners in every case computed.  Its leading parts live on the depth-two
labels of multiplicity two, $\{U-T,U-T'\}$ with $T$, $T'$ two-element sets of corners, and avoid the tops among them
($T\cap T'=\varnothing$, the multiplicity-two tops); and $\sum_c(m_c(U)-1)$ over the tops and co-tops never exceeds
$\mathrm{ex}(U)$ (up to $13$ boxes).  At $[4,3,2,1]$ and $[5,3,2,1]$ the graded dimensions are $7,3$ and $7,4$, the same
numbers as $\dim J$ and $\dim G/J$ in Section~\ref{sec:cubic}.  For three corners the leading part of the ghost is closed
and independent of $A$: with corner contents $c_1>c_2>c_3$, $Z_a$ the diagram $U$ without the two corners other than
$a$, $H(Z)=\prod_{b\in Z}[h(b)]$ and
\[
\frac{k_{\{Z_a,Z_b\}}}{d_{\{Z_a,Z_b\}}}\propto w_aw_b,\qquad w_a=H(Z_a)\,v_a,\qquad
v=\bigl([c_2-c_3],\,-[c_1-c_3],\,[c_1-c_2]\bigr),
\]
so the leading part is $\sum_{a,b}w_aw_b\,\chi_{[Z_a,Z_b]}$, the composite square of the virtual representation
$\sum_aw_aZ_a$, and $\{q\}v$ is the cross product of $(q^{c_a})$ and $(q^{-c_a})$.  The deeper coefficients are not
products (at $[4,3,1]$ degrees up to ten in $A^{-2}$ and irreducible numerators).  For four and five corners the vectors
of the three-element sets of corners lie in the top piece but span only $4$ of its $7$ and $10$ of its $21$ dimensions.
Checked: the exact count against the characters on all $355$ diagrams up to $15$ boxes and at two seeds up to $12$; the
three-row formulas on all $217$ three-row diagrams computed in these regimes (up to $30$ boxes), with $39$ values written
down before they were computed ($36$ three-row, $3$ four-corner) all correct, while one more guess, at
$(s_1,s_2,s_3)=(6,4,4)$, failed; the leading vector, found on the $30$ three-corner ghost diagrams up to $13$ boxes, on
$11$ more at $14$--$15$ boxes and at a second point on $11$ diagrams up to $19$ boxes, with a sign-flipped control; the
depth profiles at two seeds.  Not closed: $\mathrm{ex}(U)$ for four or more corners and for three rows with
$s_3\ge4$ and $\min(s_1,s_2)=3$ or $s_3\ge5$, a basis of the ghost space below the top piece, and the ghost coefficients
below the leading part.
\item \textbf{Checks}, with $Z_g$ from \eqref{eq:ghost} and nothing fitted: the formula agrees with the strengthened
Formula~II of the other branch for all $137$ diagrams with $|R|\le10$ except $[4,3,2,1]$, $m=-4,\dots,4$, two points; at
$m=-1$ this includes the stored $H_R(4_1)$ of $[4,3,1]$ and $[3,2,2,1]$, where \eqref{eq:channel} alone fails.  The
exact eight-box twist-knot polynomials of the other branch ($50$ files, $m=-5,\dots,5$) agree at a third point; they come
from the same engine and are independent only at $m=\pm1$.  The trefoil value of $Z_g$ equals the engine's
constants for $[4,3,1]$, $[5,3,1]$, $[5,4,1]$, $[6,3,1]$, $[5,3,2]$ (obtained there from figure-eight data) and its
conjectured family at $[7,3,1]$, $[8,3,1]$,
\[
Z_{[a,3,1]}=-\{q\}^{10}\,\frac{[a+2]\,[4]}{[a-4]!}\prod_{b\in[a,3,1]}[h(b)]\;D_{-3}D_{-2}D_{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{a-4}D_k
\prod_{k=a-1}^{2a-3}D_k ;
\]
and the predicted $H_U(\Tw_m)$ for $U=[7,3,1]$, $[6,3,2]$, $[7,3,2]$ are Laurent polynomials in $A$ on the twist-knot
windows for $m=-1,\pm2,3$, while without the ghost term or with $2Z_g$ they are not ($[6,3,2]$ at all four $m$,
$[7,3,2]$ at $m=2,3$).  This is a check and not a principle: polynomiality alone does not fix $Z_g$ (next items).  $Z_g$
is not a product in general: $Z_{[6,3,2]}$ has a pole at $D_1$.
\item \textbf{The top part of the layer.}  At $R=U$ write $H_U(\Tw_m)=H^{\rm low}_U(m)+\sum_XY_X(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1)$, where
$H^{\rm low}_U$ collects the vacuum and every term whose channel or ghost has its minimal diagram strictly inside $U$
(one term per minimal diagram for the split channels).  The \emph{$U$-level twist vector} $Y$ lies in $I_U$, and its
coordinates on the tops and co-tops of $U$ are fixed by the top-weight law, $Y_c=m_c(U)\,d_c\,\rho(U)$
(Observation~\ref{obs:topweight}).  So the free directions of the top rows drop out, and only the $\mathrm{ex}(U)$ ghost
coordinates of $Y$ are open.  For $U=[4,3,2,1]$: $\dim I_U=24=12+2+10$; the lower part ($118$ terms, four of them from
split channels, and the trefoil-rule ghost terms of $[4,3,1]$ and $[3,2,2,1]$) is term by term that of the closed
$[4,3,2,1]$ block of the other branch, which reproduces Formula~I for $|m|\le4$ and Rosso--Jones; and the block's $Y$ lies
in $I_U$ with the predicted top coordinates, $m_c=2$ on the three separate/separate tops (two points).  So at
$R=[4,3,2,1]$ exactly ten numbers per point are open, more than the eight twist knots of Formula~I can fix.
\item \textbf{What does not fix the layer.}  Measured at $R=U$ with the top coordinates imposed and nothing fitted
(random $A$ at a fixed $q$; the unknowns are the $A$-coefficients of $H_U(\Tw_m)$ on the twist-knot windows, and the
ghost coordinates are free at each point).  Polynomiality in $A$ alone leaves a nine-parameter family per value of $q$
already at $[4,3,1]$; the amphichirality of $4_1$ (the sweeps at $q$ and $1/q$ coupled) leaves one parameter; and
Rosso--Jones fixes it with equations to spare, an independent check of the trefoil rule at $\mathrm{ex}(U)=1$.  At
$\mathrm{ex}(U)=2$ ($[5,4,2]$, $[6,4,1]$) polynomiality, $4_1$ and Rosso--Jones together leave $34$ free coefficients, at
$[4,3,2,1]$ they leave $259$ ($43$ with Formula~I for $|m|\le4$, which lies in the family).  The reason is a count.  The
twist knots see the layer of $U$ only through a symmetric form $G^U$ on its ghost space, the ghost part of $H$ being
$\sum_{a,b}G^U_{ab}\,k_a(R)\,\phi_b(m)$ with $k_a(R)=\sum_Xk_{a,X}\psi_X(R)$, $\phi_b(m)=\sum_Xk_{b,X}(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1)$
(the ghost analogue of the matrix $G$ of Remark~(b); for $\mathrm{ex}(U)=1$ it is \eqref{eq:ghost}; by
Section~\ref{sec:cubic} it is $G^U=\frac{M(U)}{\rho(U)}K_U$, with $K_U$ the double-braid block at $R=U$).  $G^U$ has
$e(e+1)/2$ entries for $e=\mathrm{ex}(U)$.  Rosso--Jones at all $R\supseteq U$ sees only the $e$ numbers $G^U\phi(1)$,
and a closed $H_R(4_1)$ would add nothing, since $\phi(-1)=\phi(1)/M(U)$ on $I_U$ (Observation~\ref{obs:monomial}).  So
these fix the layer only for $e=1$, while $[4,3,2,1]$ has $55$ entries.
\item \textbf{The double braids do not fix it either} (analysis).  At $R=U$ the double braids see $K_U$ on all pairs
$(\phi(m),\phi(n))$, and $H_U(\mathrm{DB}(m,n))$ must be a Laurent polynomial in $A$ on its window.  The window is
$[2|U|,2(m+n)|U|]$ for $m,n>0$, its negative for $m,n<0$, and $[2\min(m,n)|U|,2\max(m,n)|U|]$ for $mn<0$, with both
ends attained: exact on all $522$ stored double-braid values up to six boxes (check \LMPH{dbraid}), and on the other
branch's values at eight to eleven boxes.  The mirror symmetry and the amphichirality of $\mathrm{DB}(m,-m)$ add no
condition, by the mirror covariance of Observation~\ref{obs:monomial}.  The counts, with the rows fixed by the zero rule
and the support principle of the other branch:
\begin{itemize}
\item at $[4,3,1]$, polynomiality of all double braids leaves one parameter and Rosso--Jones fixes it, which reproduces
the trefoil rule;
\item at $[5,4,2]$ and $[6,4,1]$ ($e=2$) it leaves $18$, and $17$ with Rosso--Jones; $11$ of these lie in the
twist-invisible square $s(A)\,u\otimes u$, $u$ the ghost direction with $\phi_u(\pm1)=0$.  One exact
$H_U(7_4)=H_U(\mathrm{DB}(2,2))$ or $H_U(8_3)=H_U(\mathrm{DB}(-2,2))$ would fix everything, while an exact $H_U(5_2)$
leaves $11$;
\item at $[4,3,2,1]$ the block of the other branch has a twist-invisible part (rank $8$ on the nine-dimensional space
$\{u:\phi_u(\pm1)=0\}$), and deforming it there changes no twist knot at $R=U$.  Polynomiality leaves $15$ parameters for
one such direction and $511$ for the deformations on the five canonical ghosts with $\phi(\pm1)=0$.
\end{itemize}
What survives is a polynomial multiple, $s(A)=s_0(A)\,p(A^2)$ with $\deg p$ bounded only by the narrowest window, which no
linear polynomiality condition removes.  So none of these conditions at $R=U$ fixes the layer for $e\ge2$.  The twist
knots at $R\supsetneq U$ do see the invisible part, since they contract $K_U$ with $f(R)$ rather than $f(U)$; their
polynomiality was not tested.
\item \textbf{What does fix it: the regularity law.}  The conditions above constrain $H$; the law of
Observation~\ref{obs:law} constrains every coefficient $w_X(R)$ separately, with the prefactor $d_X\rho(R)$ and the degree
bound $|R|-|M_X(R)|$.  Imposed at $R=U$ with the whole $V_U$ unknown (Section~\ref{sec:onepage}), it has a unique solution at
every diagram tried: at $[4,3,1]$ and $[3,2,2,1]$ it reproduces the trefoil rule, at $[5,4,2]$, $[6,4,1]$ ($e=2$) the
completion with only the layers above them unknown, and at $[4,3,2,1]$ the closed block of the other branch label by label
($128$ labels, and Formula~I $8/8$, at both points), with no Rosso--Jones or Formula~I input.  It fixes the twist-knot content of the layer
(the total $t(R)$ at each $R$), not $K_U$ as a symmetric form.
\item \textbf{Open.}  Closed formulas for the layers of the ghost diagrams with $\mathrm{ex}(U)\ge2$ (first $[6,4,1]$,
$[5,4,2]$ at eleven boxes) and of the four-corner diagrams ($[4,3,2,1]$ with ten ghosts, $[5,3,2,1]$ with eleven): the law
gives them as the solution of a linear system, not as products; the forms $K_U$ themselves; $\mathrm{ex}(U)$ outside the
three-row regimes above; the ghost coefficients below their leading part, which is closed for three corners.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Double braids and Borromean rings: one cubic tensor}\label{sec:cubic}

The twist knots are the case $n=1$ of the antiparallel double braids $\mathrm{DB}(m,n)$ ($\mathrm{DB}(m,n)=\mathrm{DB}(n,m)$,
with mirror $\mathrm{DB}(-m,-n)$), and \eqref{eq:F2} gives these from the same rows.  Two closed laws turn \eqref{eq:F2}
into the double-braid form of \eqref{eq:U}, and the same constants give the Borromean rings.  All three families are
contractions of one symmetric cubic tensor $T$ on the channels, which is how the strengthened Formula~II of the other
branch is written:
\[
H_R(\mathrm{DB}(m,n))=T(\Lam^m,\Lam^n,\chih(R)),\qquad b(R_1,R_2,R_3)=T(\chih(R_1),\chih(R_2),\chih(R_3)).
\]
Here $b$ is the reduced polynomial of the Borromean rings $L6a4$ with components coloured $R_1,R_2,R_3$, divided by
$d_{R_2}d_{R_3}$.  The twist legs $\Lam^m$ and the colour legs $\chih(R)$ meet at $\Lam^0=\chih(\varnothing)$.

\begin{observation}[monomial law and ideal identity]\label{obs:monomial}
Let $M(U)=(-1)^{|U|}A^{2|U|}q^{2\kappa_U}$.  For every diagram $U$ and every $f=\sum_Xf_X\psi_X$ in the vanishing ideal
$I_U$ (Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}), with $f(R)=\sum_Xf_X\psi_X(R)$ and $\varphi_f(m)=\sum_Xf_X(\Lam_X^{\,m}-1)$,
\[
f(U)=\rho(U)\,\varphi_f(-1),\qquad\qquad \varphi_f(1)=M(U)\,\varphi_f(-1).
\]
So on $I_U$ the evaluation at $U$ and the twist functionals at $m=\pm1$ are proportional.  Hence, in the normalisation
$\Phi(-1)=1$, $\Psi_c(R_c)=\rho(R_c)$ and $\Phi_c(1)=M(R_c)$ for every channel (Remark~(c) of
Section~\ref{sec:universal}), for every split row of a channel with several minimal diagrams ($R_c$ the minimal diagram
of the row), and for every ghost and every combination of ghosts.  The top-weight law with $m_c=1$ thus holds on the
whole ideal, and no single term $\Et_c$ can carry a multiplicity $m_c=2$.  The vacuum law also holds on all of $I_U$,
$\sum_Xf_X=-\pi(U)\varphi_f(-1)$.  Further, $\rho(U)/M(U)=\rho(U)|_{A\to1/A,\,q\to1/q}$; the $\psi_X$ and the ideals are
mirror invariant, and the rows are mirror covariant, $\Et_{cX}(1/A,1/q)=\Et_{cX}(A,q)/M(R_c)$, so
$\Phi_c(m)|_{A\to1/A,\,q\to1/q}=\Phi_c(-m)/M(R_c)$.
\end{observation}

\begin{conjecture}[cubic form]\label{conj:cubic}
Take one functional $\Et_c$ for each channel and each of its minimal diagrams (the split rows), and one for each ghost.
Then $\Et_c(\chih(R))=\Psi_c(R)=\rho(R_c)\cf Rc$ and $\Et_c(\Lam^m)=\Phi_c(m)$, and
\begin{gather*}
T=\sum_ct_c\,\Et_c^{\otimes3}+\sum_{U\ \mathrm{deferred}}\tau_U\,\mathrm{Sym}(h_0^U\otimes h_0^U\otimes h_1^U)
\;\bigl(+\text{the block of }[4,3,2,1]\bigr),\\
t_c=\frac{d_cN_{cc}}{M(R_c)}=\frac{Z_c}{\rho(R_c)M(R_c)},
\end{gather*}
with $\mathrm{Sym}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=a\otimes b\otimes c+b\otimes c\otimes a+c\otimes a\otimes b$ and $\tau_U$, $h_0^U$,
$h_1^U$ as below.  Explicitly, for $|R|\le10$,
\begin{align}
H_R(\mathrm{DB}(m,n))&=\sum_c\frac{Z_c}{M(R_c)}\,\cf Rc\,\Phi_c(m)\,\Phi_c(n)
+\sum_{U\ \mathrm{deferred},\,U\subseteq R}\frac{Z^{\rm def}_U}{M(U)}\,\cf R{F_1^U}\,\delta\Phi_U(m)\,\delta\Phi_U(n),
\label{eq:DB}\\
b(R_1,R_2,R_3)&=1+\sum_cZ_c\,\frac{\rho(R_c)^2}{M(R_c)}\,\cf{R_1}c\,\cf{R_2}c\,\cf{R_3}c, \label{eq:borr}
\end{align}
where $c$ runs over the channels and ghosts of $R$ in \eqref{eq:DB}, and in \eqref{eq:borr} over those present at all
three colours.  Note that this set is not the set of channels of $R_1\cap R_2\cap R_3$: $\{[1,1],[2]\}$ is present at $[3,2]$
and at $[2,2,1]$ but not at $[2,2]$.
\end{conjecture}

For $n=1$, \eqref{eq:DB} is \eqref{eq:U}, because $\Phi_c(1)=M(R_c)$ and the deferred terms vanish there.  The constants are
closed wherever $Z_c$ is: $Z_c/M(R_c)=d_cN_{cc}\,\rho(R_c)|_{A\to1/A,\,q\to1/q}$.  The ghost terms of \eqref{eq:U} are
cubes of the same form, $(Z_g/M(U))\cf Rg\Phi_g(m)\Phi_g(n)$.  These are the ``kernel cubes'' of the other branch.  Its
split channels are our split rows, and its kernel cube at $[5,3,1,1]$ (where $\mathrm{ex}=0$) is absorbed by our row of
$\{[3,2,1,1],[5,2]\}$ on the enlarged support.

The mirror covariance gives the \emph{mirror identity}.  Since $\mathrm{DB}(n,-1)$ is the mirror of $\Tw_{-n}$,
\[
\sum_c\frac{Z_c}{M(R_c)}\cf Rc\,\Phi_c(n)=H_R(\Tw_{-n})\big|_{A\to1/A,\,q\to1/q}.
\]
This is an identity between the twist-knot formula and itself, and it holds term by term by the mirror covariance.

(\eqref{eq:fourrow}, \eqref{eq:adjres}, \eqref{eq:roots}, \eqref{eq:Nsingle}, \eqref{eq:Bsingle}).  For the non-resonant
two-cell pairs the single columns (kernel \eqref{eq:kernel}), the diagonal, the first pair column, the pair columns of
the top weight and the zeros are closed (Observation~\ref{obs:twocell}); the light columns are explicit Lagrange
interpolants \eqref{eq:lagrange}, ratios of determinants rather than products (the pure-$q$ property behind
\eqref{eq:roots} fails there).  The hook pair columns, the one-row and one-column singles, the two-column axis columns on
their family and the weight-four two-cell columns are closed (Observation~\ref{obs:interp}); open is a formula for the
other columns of the Lagrange basis, first the one-cell columns not of axis type (from weight $10$ on the basis lives on
the power sums restricted to the channels).  The resonant two-cell pairs (vertical against horizontal domino, e.g.\
$\{[1,1,1],[3]\}$ in $R=[3,2,1]$) have closed single columns on $[V,R_{\min}]$, products in the heavy pair columns, and
light columns that are Lagrange interpolants on an enlarged space with closed product coordinates
(Observation~\ref{obs:resrows}); they are fixed by the vanishing conditions, while the twist-knot data test only their
eigenvalue class (Observation~\ref{obs:kernel}).  Line pairs are closed on the single columns except the deep states for
$n\ge6$; the three-cell line rows follow the same interpolation rule, but their three-cell block is not closed (three of
its $27$ entries in the two-row world are not products).  For three or more moved cells per side the diagonal (one
effective shape, or a line side), the heavy support of the line rows and their two partners, and half of the kernel
states of a domino plus a separate cell against a line are closed (Observation~\ref{obs:threecell}); the other heavy
columns (for $n=3$ the middle chain pair is not a product), the pair columns of the rows that are not line rows, the
other kernel states, bent sides beyond the diagonal, the anomalous pairs ($m_c(U)=0$) and the separate/separate pairs
(no data) are open.
\item \textbf{Non-top evolution coefficients}: product $\times$ polynomial, polynomial not closed
(Section~\ref{sec:weights}); already $w_{[1]}([2])$.  The regularity law (Observation~\ref{obs:law}) fixes the product,
$d_X\rho(R)$, and the degree of the polynomial, $|R|-|M_X(R)|$, and above the ghosts it determines the coefficients
through a linear system; a closed formula for the polynomial $K_X(R)$ itself is open.
\item \textbf{Resonances}: from $[3,2,1]$ on, the evolution form does not separate channels with equal eigenvalues;
the channel form does.
\item \textbf{Rank deficiency}: with the engine's default node bound, some vanishing systems have free parameters
from seven boxes on.  This is an artefact of the node bound for all of them except the five top channels of $R=[4,3,1]$
(the single $[4,3,1]$ and the pairs $\{[3,3],[4,1,1]\}$, $\{[4,2,1],[4,3]\}$, $\{[3,3,1],[4,3]\}$, $\{[3,3,1],[4,2,1]\}$)
and the five of $[3,2,2,1]$: with the conditions on all diagrams with at most $13$ boxes every other row is determined
(and agrees with \eqref{eq:tab}, \eqref{eq:union} where those apply), while these ten keep a common one-dimensional
kernel.  No value of the free parameter makes \eqref{eq:channel} reproduce $H_R(\Tw_m)$ for these two $R$; the
missing term is the ghost term of \eqref{eq:U}, a rank-one term built from the kernel vector, with its constant fixed by
the trefoil (Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}; the strengthened Formula II of the other branch contains the same term).  At nine
boxes the same happens for the tops of $[5,3,1]$, $[3,2,2,1,1]$, and four channels with two minimal diagrams are not
determined either.  With the ghost terms everything up to ten boxes is accounted for except $[4,3,2,1]$; open were the
ghost diagrams with two or more ghosts (from eleven boxes) and the four-corner diagrams.  For them only the ghost
coordinates are open (the tops follow from the top-weight law with multiplicities, ten numbers per point remain for
$[4,3,2,1]$), and none of the principles tested (polynomiality in $A$, the amphichirality of $4_1$, Rosso--Jones) fixes
them once $\mathrm{ex}(U)\ge2$, and neither do the double braids at $R=U$ with their mirror symmetry
(Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}).  What the twist knots see of such a diagram at every $R\supseteq U$ is one symmetric form, its
double-braid block $K_U$ at $R=U$ (Section~\ref{sec:cubic}); one exact double braid with $|m|,|n|\ge2$ at $R=U$ would fix
it at $\mathrm{ex}(U)=2$.  The regularity law fixes the twist-knot content of all of them at once
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:final}): it is a conjecture, checked on every diagram it was run on (Table~\ref{tab:final}), and
it gives the layers as the solution of a linear system, not in closed form.  The number of ghosts has an exact form (one super-Cauchy identity per corner) and is closed for
three rows with a gap at most two or $s_3=3$, not in general; for three corners the leading part of the ghost is closed
(Section~\ref{sec:ghosts}).
\item \textbf{Double braids and Borromean rings} (Section~\ref{sec:cubic}): the cubic form is checked up to ten boxes
and away from $[4,3,2,1]$ only; the factors $C_U$ and $\sigma_U$ of the deferred constants are those fitted by the other
branch (new here are $g_U$, $a_U$ and the identification of $h_0$, $h_1$ with our rows); at eleven boxes the nine-box
deferred terms should enter the twist knots at the five $R\supsetneq[4,3,2,1]$, which is not tested.  The rule
$G^U=\frac{M(U)}{\rho(U)}K_U$ follows from the ideal identity; it is checked against the other branch's own eleven-box
files only.  In the block of $[4,3,2,1]$ every entry is closed except the ghost part $Q_G$, a finite computation
from the other branch's Gram matrix; the part of the block that the twist knots do not see is the other branch's
hypothesis, and the double braids at $R\supsetneq[4,3,2,1]$ depend on it.
\item \textbf{The regularity law}: that its solution is unique is observed (every diagram tried, up to twelve boxes),
not shown; the vanishing ideals are computed with the conditions on diagrams up to a node bound ($|R|+4$, at least
$15$, and $|R|+6$ from eleven boxes on, where a smaller bound leaves spurious directions); with less input than the
channels below the ghosts it is not unique (Section~\ref{sec:onepage}, remark (d)).
\item \textbf{Nothing is proved}: Conjectures \ref{conj:main}, \ref{conj:kntz}, \ref{conj:tab}, \ref{conj:U},
\ref{conj:cubic}, \ref{conj:final} and all Observations are statements verified on the data listed above only.
\end{enumerate}
